    How to Protect Your Pipes Before the Next Kansas Freeze

    By Andrew Blake · October 6, 2026 · 5 min read

    Understanding the Kansas Freeze Risk

    Hard freezes happen every winter in Kansas, and pipes in exterior walls, garages, and crawlspaces are the first to burst.

    Weather is often unpredictable, and a mild afternoon can turn into a hard freeze overnight. Those sudden drops in temperature can do real damage to your home's plumbing. Homes built for long, deep winters tend to have deeply buried pipes and thorough insulation, but many homes, especially older ones, weren't designed to withstand prolonged freezing conditions. That means exposed pipes, both inside and outside your home, are vulnerable to freezing and bursting, leading to significant water damage and expensive repairs.

    The issue isn't just the cold, but the rapid temperature swings. When water freezes, it expands. This expansion puts immense pressure on your pipes, and even the toughest copper or PEX lines can eventually burst. Knowing which pipes are most at risk, like those in unheated garages, crawl spaces, or along exterior walls, is the first step in smart prevention.

    Essential Steps to Prepare Your Outdoor Plumbing

    Your outdoor plumbing is often the first to feel the chill. Disconnecting garden hoses is essential. Leaving them attached can trap water in the hose and spigot, which will freeze and expand, possibly damaging the faucet and the pipe it connects to. Once hoses are off, drain any remaining water from the spigots. Many outdoor faucets, known as sillcocks, are freeze-resistant, but they still need protection in extreme cold.

    Consider insulating your outdoor spigots with foam covers, readily available at any hardware store. These simple covers can make a big difference. For irrigation systems, it's vital to have them correctly winterized. This usually involves blowing out the lines with an air compressor to remove all water. If you're not comfortable doing this yourself, a landscaping or irrigation specialist can take care of it for you. Neglecting your sprinkler system can lead to costly repairs when spring rolls around.

    Protecting Interior and Exposed Pipes

    Inside your home, focus on pipes that run along exterior walls, in unheated basements, or in attics. These are likely candidates for freezing. One of the simplest and most effective methods is to insulate these pipes. Foam pipe insulation sleeves are inexpensive and easy to install. Just cut them to size and secure them around the pipes. For pipes in crawl spaces or attics, ensure any access points are sealed to prevent cold air from circulating.

    During a hard freeze, it's also a good idea to open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls. This allows warmer indoor air to move around the pipes, helping to prevent freezing. For really bitter cold, a slow drip from your faucets, especially those on exterior walls, can keep water moving through the pipes, significantly reducing the risk of freezing. Just a trickle is enough to prevent pressure buildup.

    • Insulate exposed pipes: Use foam pipe insulation sleeves for pipes in unheated spaces.
    • Disconnect and drain hoses: Remove all garden hoses and drain outdoor spigots.
    • Cover outdoor faucets: Install foam covers on every exterior hose bib.
    • Open cabinet doors: Allow warm air to reach pipes under sinks on exterior walls.
    • Drip faucets: During severe cold, a slow drip keeps water flowing and prevents freezing.
    • Seal air leaks: Caulk or weatherstrip around windows, doors, and foundation cracks to keep cold air out.

    When to Call a Professional

    Sometimes, despite your best efforts, you might still encounter a frozen pipe. If you suspect a pipe is frozen, or if you've already experienced a burst pipe, it's crucial to act quickly. A small leak can quickly turn into a major flood, causing extensive damage to your home's structure, flooring, and personal belongings. If you discover a frozen pipe, you can try to thaw it slowly with a hairdryer or heat lamp, but never use an open flame.

    However, if you can't locate the frozen section, or if a pipe has burst, it's time to call in the experts. KRV Plumbing is ready to help across Kansas.     Preventative Maintenance and Inspections

    Beyond immediate freeze preparations, consider a professional plumbing inspection. A KRV Plumbing plumber can identify vulnerable pipes, recommend permanent insulation solutions, and check for any existing issues that could worsen during a freeze. This proactive approach can save you from the stress and expense of emergency repairs when the next cold snap hits.
