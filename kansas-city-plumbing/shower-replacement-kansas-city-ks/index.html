    Shower Unit Replacement in Kansas City

    Cracked fiberglass units leak at the base first. Cracks tend to start where the base was not supported, and each shower flexes it until water slips through to the subfloor. Out comes the old one. Then we check the subfloor and set the new base on a full mortar bed.

    Tub-to-Shower Replacement in Kansas City

    Old bathtubs that nobody uses take up the best spot in the bathroom. We remove the tub, reroute the drain to the new pan location and install the valve at shower height, then set walls and a door or glass panel. Stepping in gets easier. It is safer for everyone.

    Custom Tile Shower Replacement in Kansas City, KS

    Custom tile replacement starts with careful demolition. Demo goes to the studs, damaged wood gets replaced and a new waterproofed pan goes in with the valve set to tile depth. Slope matters most. A flood test comes before tile.

    Shower Replacement Options and Costs in Kansas City, KS

    A valve-only swap costs far less than a full rebuild. It only makes sense when the pan and walls are sound, though, because a new valve in a shower with a failing pan just moves the leak to a different spot. New pans usually mean new tile. We match the option to the shower's condition.

    Shower Pan Replacement in Kansas City, KS

    Cracked pans damage subfloors, walls and nearby rooms. On slab homes, water can travel under the flooring and show up as staining or odors in a hallway, so we trace it before we start tearing anything out. Then we remove the pan. The new pan gets proper slope, waterproofing and a flood test.

    Aging-in-Place Shower Replacement in Kansas City

    Aging in place often starts with getting rid of the tub. The tub comes out, the drain is adjusted and the valve goes in at the right height with grab bar blocking and a handheld. Thresholds stay low. It is one of the safest changes you can make.

    Prefab Shower Replacement in Kansas City

    Prefab units past their useful life usually show it at the base. Fading, hairline cracks and a soft spot underfoot all mean water has been getting through, and we check the subfloor and the wall framing once the old unit is out. Then the new unit goes in. It goes on mortar with a sealed drain.
