    Wall Waterproofing Work We Handle in Kansas City, KS

    Foundation Crack Repair in Kansas City

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack. We set injection ports every 8 to 12 inches along the crack, seal the surface with epoxy paste, and pump in resin from the bottom port up until it pushes out the next port. Polyurethane stays flexible if the wall moves a little, and epoxy bonds the two sides back together. The surface paste gets ground off once it cures.

    Shower Pan Leak Repair and Waterproofing in Kansas City, KS

    Wet baseboards outside the shower or a stain on the ceiling below usually point to the pan. We plug the drain, fill the pan with an inch or two of water, and mark the waterline and check it a day later. A drop means a leak. Rebuilding means a mortar pre-slope of a quarter inch per foot, a new PVC liner or bonded membrane, and a clamping drain tightened onto the liner. We flood test the new pan before any tile goes down.

    Plumbing Leak Behind Walls in Kansas City

    A drip from a shower valve cartridge, a sweated copper stub or a loose drain slip nut can soak a wall cavity for months before paint bubbles. We find it with a moisture meter, a thermal camera that shows the cool wet spot, and a pressure test on the supply lines. Then we cut a clean access hole. We replace the leaking fitting or pipe section. We pull wet insulation and run fans until the cavity reads dry.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Kansas City

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and usually add a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60 percent. Strong winds and dry spells can pull moisture out of the soil, while sudden cold fronts can drop temperatures sharply overnight. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also cuts down on the musty smell in the house.

    Exterior Block Wall Waterproofing in Kansas City, KS

    White crust on block or stem walls is efflorescence, salt left behind as water moves through the masonry and dries. The usual cause is a sprinkler head spraying the wall instead of the plants, or soil graded toward the foundation. We fix the water first. Then we clean the wall, let it dry and apply a breathable masonry sealer or elastomeric coating.

    Cost-Effective Waterproofing Solutions in Kansas City, KS

    Not every wet wall needs a $15,000 excavation. Sometimes the fix is a downspout extension and regrading the soil so it slopes away from the house, a drop of about six inches in the first ten feet, and that costs a few hundred dollars. We start with the cheapest fix that will actually work. Every option comes written out with its price.

    Drainage System Integration in Kansas City, KS

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a downspout emptying right at the foundation. We extend downspouts at least four feet from the wall.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Kansas City

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, overlapped a foot at every seam and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and hold it in place with termination strip. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Anywhere from an afternoon to over a week. Interior crack sealing might take a day or two, while exterior excavation with new footing drains can take several days to more than a week depending on the size of the house. Wet weather can push exterior work back.

    A shower wall job usually takes two to four days from demolition to finished membrane, including the drying time between coats. Exterior block sealing often wraps up in a day. Larger foundation jobs can run several days, and weather can stretch that.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, which is what a basement in wet soil or a high water table needs.

    Always. Sealing a wall with an active leak inside it traps the water, so the studs and sheathing rot faster than if the wall had been left alone. The leak gets repaired and the cavity dried before anything else.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide since soil movement, groundwater levels and how well it was installed all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Drain tile can silt up over time.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, using drain tile under the slab edge and a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. The exterior route costs more.
