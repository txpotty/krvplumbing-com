    Repiping and Pipe Replacement Options in Kansas City, KS

    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Kansas City

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. It degrades from the inside. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Kansas City, KS

    We often leave old galvanized pipe in the wall. It is disconnected and capped, while new lines run beside it, so we avoid tearing open walls just to pull out dead pipe. Only accessible pipe comes out.

    Copper Pipe Replacement for Older Homes in Kansas City, KS

    Older homes sometimes have copper that is thin from decades of use. Small leaks appear in random spots. We replace it with new Type L copper or PEX, matching the layout or improving it where the old routing was awkward. Better flow.

    Pipe Replacement for Multi-Unit Buildings in Kansas City

    Apartments and condos share risers. Shared lines mean shared problems. We plan riser replacement in phases, one stack at a time, keeping each unit informed. Organized work.

    Full Repipes for Older Homes in Kansas City

    Homes from the 1950s and 1960s often still have original galvanized pipe. It is near the end. A full repipe brings new PEX or copper to every fixture, and fixes pressure and water color at once. Worth it.

    Whole-Home Repiping in Kansas City

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. You can usually stay in the house. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, then patch every opening before we leave. Clean finish.

    Copper Pipe Replacement in Kansas City, KS

    Copper still has fans. It handles heat well and lasts decades. We install Type L copper for most interior supply lines, sweat or press the joints, and support every run with hangers at the spacing code requires, so the new pipe does not rattle or sag. Costs more than PEX.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in Kansas City

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. We locate the leak first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, and rerouting often costs less and avoids future slab leaks on that line.
