    Gas Leak Detection in Kansas City

    Some leaks announce themselves with a hiss you can hear across the room, and others lose a few cubic feet a day and only show up on the bill. The slow ones are where the gauge earns its keep. We isolate, then sniff. Testing happens with the appliance valves shut and again with them open, since some leaks hide past a valve. Finding the exact joint keeps the job small.

    Emergency Gas Leak Repair in Kansas City, KS

    Hissing at the meter, a sulfur smell in the kitchen, a dead patch of lawn or a carbon monoxide alarm all mean the same thing: get everyone outside before you touch anything. From the yard or a neighbor's porch, call the gas utility first. KRV Plumbing runs emergency gas calls in Kansas City, KS around the clock. Once the utility has shut the meter, our tech isolates the leaking branch and makes the repair. Unaffected appliances usually come back the same day.

    Propane Gas Leak Repair in Kansas City, KS

    Propane behaves differently from natural gas because it is heavier than air, so a leak settles into basements and crawlspaces instead of rising. The first-stage regulator at the tank and the second-stage regulator at the house both get checked. Low spots get sniffed first. If the supplier owns the tank, they handle the tank side and we handle the piping from there to the appliances. Rural properties with buried tanks get a line test too.

    Residential Gas Leak Detection in Kansas City

    A gas leak in a house rarely sits where the smell is strongest, because gas drifts along joists and collects in closets and basements. The first step is a pressure gauge on the piping, which tells us whether the system is actually losing gas. Then the sniffer comes out. A combustible gas detector follows the reading from fitting to fitting until it spikes. Bubble solution on the suspect joint gives the final confirmation.

    Appliance Gas Line Repair in Kansas City, KS

    Most appliance leaks show up at the flex connector or the shutoff valve behind the appliance, usually right at a flare nut where the two meet. Connectors get bent every time a range is pulled out to clean behind it. Valves weep at the stem. The fix is usually a new coated connector and a new ball valve, plus a drip leg if the appliance lacks one. Each joint gets a detector pass and a bubble test before the pilot is relit.

    Gas Line Replacement and Upgrades in Kansas City, KS

    Adding a gas range or a tankless heater can overload pipe that was sized years ago for just a furnace and a water heater. Sizing starts with the total BTU load and the longest run from the meter. Undersized pipe starves burners. Where the old pipe is rusty or poorly threaded, we replace it with black iron or CSST, whichever reaches easier. A big tankless unit may also need a bigger meter from the utility.

    Red Tag Gas Repairs in Kansas City

    A red tag on your meter or appliance means the utility found a leak or unsafe condition and will not restore gas until a licensed contractor fixes it. We read the tag first, since it lists what the utility found. Then we test everything. After the repair, we run the pressure test the utility or inspector requires and send them the paperwork. Relights are scheduled with the utility, usually within a day.

    Appliance Connector Replacement in Kansas City

    Old uncoated brass connectors, the kind made before the mid-1970s, are the subject of consumer safety warnings because the flare nut can separate from the tube. If your range or water heater still has one, it should come out. We see them weekly. In its place goes a coated stainless connector and a new shutoff valve, plus a drip leg if the code calls for it. Bubble testing follows before the pilot is lit.
