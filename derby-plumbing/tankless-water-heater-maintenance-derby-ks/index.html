    Inlet Filter and Air Intake Cleaning in Derby

    Every tankless unit has an inlet water screen that catches grit and scale flakes. Those screens can clog with sand and debris, especially after municipal line work. We pull and clean the screen and inspect the air intake filter, which can load up with dust and dryer lint. Clean filters keep the flow sensor reading correctly and the burner breathing properly.

    Noritz Tankless Water Heater Tune-Ups in Derby, KS

    Noritz tankless water heaters are sturdy systems, but they still require annual tune-ups to maintain their performance through every season. Our maintenance includes checking the unit's exhaust system, confirming proper combustion air intake and verifying the integrity of the heat exchanger. We also calibrate the temperature and flow settings for the best efficiency and comfort, preventing common issues like cold water sandwiches.

    Error Code Diagnostics in Derby, KS

    Codes like Rinnai LC, Navien E003 or Noritz 90 tell us a lot about what is going wrong. We read the code history, test flame rods, flow sensors and igniters and check gas pressure at the inlet. Many codes trace back to scale or a dirty filter and clear after maintenance. When a part has failed we explain the repair and the cost before doing any work.

    Heat Exchanger Descaling in Derby

    The heat exchanger in a tankless heater is a coil of small tubes that water races through while the burner fires. Hard water drops calcium and magnesium on those hot surfaces, narrowing the passages over time. We connect a submersible pump and hoses to the service valves and circulate descaling solution for 45 to 60 minutes. Then we flush with fresh water to rinse the system thoroughly. Restored flow means steadier temperatures and fewer shutdowns.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Tankless Water Heater Maintenance in Derby

    The cost for tankless water heater maintenance in Derby, KS generally ranges from $150 to $300, depending on the unit's age, condition and the extent of descaling required. This includes a full flush and inspection of critical components. More complex issues or additional repairs would be quoted separately.

    The descaling process involves shutting off the unit, isolating the water lines and connecting a circulating pump to flush a mild acidic solution through the heat exchanger. This solution dissolves mineral deposits like calcium and magnesium, which are common in hard water, restoring heat transfer efficiency.

    Neglecting maintenance can lead to several problems, including reduced heating efficiency, higher utility bills, decreased hot water output and eventually, costly breakdowns. Mineral buildup can seriously restrict water flow and damage the heat exchanger, potentially leading to premature unit failure and expensive repairs or replacement.

    A cold water sandwich can come from restricted flow, a dirty inlet filter or a flow sensor not reading correctly. Descaling and filter cleaning often improve it.

    While some basic tasks like cleaning external filters might be possible for a homeowner, complete maintenance, especially descaling the heat exchanger and inspecting gas lines, should be left to a licensed professional. Improper servicing can damage the unit, void warranties and pose safety risks related to gas and carbon monoxide.

    Common signs include reduced hot water flow, fluctuating water temperatures, odd noises like banging or popping, error codes displayed on the unit, or a noticeable increase in your energy bills. If you observe any of these symptoms, it's time to schedule a professional inspection and maintenance.

    Yes, regular maintenance is crucial for maintaining your tankless water heater's energy efficiency. Descaling removes mineral deposits that force the unit to work harder, using more energy to heat water. Clean components and proper calibration ensure your unit operates at its designed efficiency, saving you money on energy bills.

    Many manufacturers exclude scale damage from heat exchanger warranties. Regular, documented maintenance is the best way to keep your coverage intact.
