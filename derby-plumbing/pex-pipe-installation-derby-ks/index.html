    PEX Main Water Line Installation in Derby

    The main from the meter to the house feeds every fixture you own. A leaking old service line shows up as a soggy patch in the yard, a higher water bill and weak pressure everywhere. We trench and lay new PEX. At the foundation we install a new full-port ball valve as the main shutoff.

    PEX-B Crimp and Clamp Systems in Derby

    PEX-B is stiffer, costs less per foot, and joins with copper crimp rings or stainless cinch clamps over brass or poly-alloy fittings. Since a crimp is only as good as the tool that made it, every ring gets a go/no-go gauge check, and any ring that fails gets cut off and redone. That takes seconds. Skipping it causes most crimp leaks.

    PEX for Remodels & Additions in Derby, KS

    Opening a wall for a kitchen remodel is the cheapest time to swap out the old pipe. With the studs bare, we can run PEX back to a manifold or to the nearest good trunk, set new stub-outs for the sink and dishwasher, and add a line for the fridge ice maker. Additions get their own sizing. A new bath at the far end of the house may need a three-quarter inch feed to keep pressure up.

    Leak Repair with PEX in Derby, KS

    A single bad section is not always a reason to repipe. For one split or pitted stretch of copper, we cut it out, transition to PEX with a listed fitting, and usually restore water within a couple of hours. That works for one leak. By the third leak, though, we will talk to you honestly about whether the rest of that copper is close behind.

    Polybutylene Replacement With PEX in Derby, KS

    Gray polybutylene went into millions of homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. Chlorine in treated water attacks the plastic and the acetal fittings from the inside, so it can look fine from outside right up to the day a joint lets go. Many insurers ask whether you have it. Swapping it for PEX can help with insurance and with resale.

    PEX-A vs. PEX-B Installation in Derby

    Homeowners ask us which one is better, and it really depends on the house. PEX-A bends tighter, recovers from a kink with a heat gun, and its expansion fittings flow more water, while PEX-B costs less and its crimp joints are just as reliable when they are gauged properly. Either one lasts for decades. For tight attics we lean toward PEX-A.

    Copper to PEX Conversions in Derby

    Pinhole leaks in copper tend to come in clusters. Aggressive water, fast flow and soil contact under slabs can pit copper from the inside, and the first few pinholes are typically followed by more. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Converting to PEX stops the cycle. Where new PEX meets existing copper stubs or valves, we use listed transition fittings.

    Uponor PEX System Installation in Derby

    Uponor's ProPEX system is PEX-A joined with an expansion tool and a cold-expansion ring. The tech expands the pipe and ring together, pushes the fitting in, and the plastic shrinks back around the barb over the next few seconds, tighter than when it started. No crimp tool to calibrate. The fittings have a wide bore, so flow loss through each joint stays low.
