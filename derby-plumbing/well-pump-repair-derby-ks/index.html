    Submersible Well Pump Repair in Derby

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Grundfos Well Pump Repair in Derby

    Grundfos SQ and SQE pumps have built-in electronics that protect the motor and report faults. Sensor failures and drive errors are the usual problems. KRV Plumbing reads the fault code, checks the wiring and tests the pressure sensor before replacing anything in a Derby, KS well. Electronics fail less often than people think.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Derby

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. Proper sizing adds years to a pump.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Derby, KS

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Most failures start with an air leak somewhere in the suction line. KRV Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and confirms it builds pressure. A jet pump that runs nonstop without reaching pressure has almost always lost prime or sucked air through a cracked fitting.

    Control Box and Surge Repair in Derby, KS

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Severe thunderstorms are common in Kansas, and heavy downpours can overwhelm gutters, storm drains, and older sewer lines. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection, replace what failed and recommend a surge arrestor at the box if one is missing. An arrestor costs little.
