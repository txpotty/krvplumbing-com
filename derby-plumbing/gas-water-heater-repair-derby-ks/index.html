    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Derby

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. We pull the anode, check how much magnesium or aluminum is left and replace it if the core wire is showing. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Derby home that has not started leaking from the shell. A wet bottom seam is different. At that point the tank has to go.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Derby, KS

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. We read the code, then test the thermopile and sensors before blaming the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. We leave the old valve with you if you ask.

    Burner Tray and Venting Repair in Derby

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. The burner comes out, the ports get cleared and the flame arrestor gets a look. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Derby home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    All Major Gas Water Heater Brands Serviced in Derby

    Every brand label we see in garages and closets uses one of a handful of gas control designs. Rheem and Ruud share most parts, and State shares a lot with A.O. Smith. Bradford White runs its own control system, so we stock those separately. Knowing which family a heater belongs to lets the tech bring the right valve, thermocouple or igniter on the first trip instead of ordering parts and coming back two days later. Model and serial numbers help.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Derby

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Pilot Light Repair in Derby

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. A millivolt meter tells us in about a minute whether it is producing enough current. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Figure on roughly an hour for most pilot repairs.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Issues in Derby, KS

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A lot of the time it is the drain valve, the T&P valve or a dripping supply fitting. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Derby, KS home. If the bottom seam is wet, the tank has failed. Nothing patches that.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Derby, KS

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. Sections that pull apart, rust through or sag let combustion gas spill into the room. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. Spillage gets fixed before we leave.
