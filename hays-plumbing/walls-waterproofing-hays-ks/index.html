    Foundation Crack Repair & Sealing in Hays

    Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls leak more water than their size suggests.     Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Hays

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most reliable fix for a leaking basement, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing to carry the water away from the wall. Basements are common in the eastern part of Kansas, while slab and crawlspace homes are more typical in many other areas. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Hays, KS

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Strong winds and dry spells can draw moisture out of the soil, while sudden cold fronts can drop temperatures sharply overnight. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then seal the face with a masonry waterproofer that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.

    Laundry Room and Utility Wall Waterproofing in Hays, KS

    Rubber washing machine hoses burst. Water heaters get a pan too. The wall behind them can take a moisture-resistant board and sealer.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Hays

    Crack injection runs about $300 to $800 per crack. A full exterior dig and membrane on a basement usually lands between $8,000 and $20,000, depending on depth, wall length and what sits against the house, while interior drain tile with a sump typically costs $4,000 to $12,000. Prices vary with access. Each quote is itemized after we inspect.

    Crack repairs take a few hours. Interior drain tile usually takes one to three days, and a full exterior dig around the whole house can run three to seven days or more depending on the weather and the depth of the footing. Your timeline comes with the quote.

    Anywhere from an afternoon to over a week. Interior crack sealing might take a day or two, while exterior excavation with new footing drains can take several days to more than a week depending on the size of the house. Rain delays exterior work.

    Damp proofing is usually a thin spray-on asphalt coat. It slows soil moisture but will not stop water that is pushing against the wall under pressure. Waterproofing uses thicker membranes, drainage boards and footing drains rated to stop liquid water, and that is what a basement sitting in wet soil or a high water table calls for.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, using drain tile under the slab edge and a sump pump. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. Outside costs more.

    Done right with good materials, a waterproofing system lasts 20 to 50 years. The range is wide since soil movement, groundwater levels and how well it was installed all play a part, and a crack that opens later can let water around even a good membrane. Over time, drain tile can fill with silt.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before we seal anything, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, heavy equipment, correct product thickness and an understanding of how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. Done wrong, it can trap water against the wall and cause more damage.
