    Hard Water Symptoms and Diagnostics in Hays

    Scale on faucets means trouble. KRV Plumbing checks hardness at the tap, inspects every component and gives you a repair plan for your Hays home. We test hardness before and after the repair, so you see the grains per gallon fall.

    Resin Bed Problems in Hays

    Fouled resin is the usual reason a softener regenerates on schedule but still sends hard water to every faucet in the house. Iron and sediment coat the beads and block the exchange. Depending on the test, the resin gets cleaned or replaced. Fresh resin brings capacity back.

    Bypass and Fitting Leak Repair in Hays, KS

    Bypass valves, the O-rings where the valve meets the tank, cracked fittings and the drain line are the usual leak points. A minor drip still wastes water and damages floors and cabinets. O-rings and fittings get replaced and pressure tested. Mineral tank cracks cannot be fixed.

    Control Valve Rebuild in Hays

    Service, backwash, brine and rinse are the four cycles the control valve directs every time the softener regenerates, usually in the middle of the night. Hard water and chlorine wear out the seals, spacers and pistons. Leaks at the valve or water running to the drain are common signs. A matching rebuild kit fixes it.

    Emergency Water Softener Repair in Hays

    A softener stuck in regeneration can run water to the drain for hours or flood a utility room. We handle these calls quickly. The crew in Hays puts the unit in bypass first, then finds out whether the valve, the timer or the drain line caused it.     Salt Bridge and Brine Tank Service in Hays, KS

    The tank looks full but makes no brine. A salt bridge has formed and stopped the salt from dissolving. Bridges get broken up, mushy salt gets cleaned out and the float, brine well and safety valve get checked. In a hot or damp garage, switching to pellets cuts down on bridging.

    Water Softener Repair vs. Replacement in Hays, KS

    Age and parts availability decide whether a softener is worth repairing. KRV Plumbing gives honest advice on both counts. A ten-year-old unit with a sound tank and a rebuildable valve is usually worth fixing, while an obsolete head with no parts available is a different conversation. We lay out both options.
