    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Hays

    A standard sump pump replacement typically costs $500 to $1,200 installed. Battery backup systems usually add $800 to $1,800. Sewage ejector pumps generally cost $1,200 to $2,500.

    A battery backup sump pump system is a secondary pump powered by a battery, designed to turn on automatically if your primary sump pump loses power during an outage or fails. This is important for maintaining flood protection during power outages caused by severe weather.

    Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. Pumps that sit idle for long periods can fail earlier due to stuck switches.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump at least every three months, and especially before and after rainy seasons. To test, simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit. The pump should activate, drain the water and then shut off automatically. If it does not, it is time for a professional inspection.

    To maintain your new sump pump, regularly check the pit for debris, test the pump monthly by pouring water into the pit, and make sure the discharge pipe is clear. An annual professional inspection by KRV Plumbing can also help extend its lifespan.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a smart investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    Key signs include the pump not turning on, unusual noises such as grinding or rattling, running nonstop, visible rust or corrosion, or an age over 7 to 10 years. Any of these could point to coming failure and a need for replacement.

    Most sump pump replacements take one to two hours. Battery backups or new discharge lines take longer.
