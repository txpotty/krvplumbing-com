    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Leawood, KS

    The float switch is the most common reason a sump pump fails to start. Tethered floats can tangle on the pump body, and vertical floats can stick from mineral crust or debris. We clean or replace the switch and set the on and off levels so the pump does not short cycle. Electronic sensor switches are an option for tight pits where floats get stuck.

    Battery Backup Pump Maintenance in Leawood

    Severe storms often bring power outages along with the rain. A battery backup pump keeps water moving when the main pump loses power or fails. We load test the battery, check the charger and alarm and run the backup pump on its own. Most backup batteries need replacing every three to five years.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Maintenance in Leawood

    The cost for sump pump maintenance in Leawood, KS typically ranges from $100 to $250, depending on the pump type and its condition. This price usually includes a thorough inspection, cleaning and operational testing. Any necessary repairs or parts replacement would be an additional cost, which we would quote upfront.

    While you can perform basic checks like making sure the pump is plugged in and the discharge pipe isn't blocked, professional maintenance goes much deeper. Our technicians can spot subtle electrical issues, float switch malfunctions or impeller damage that a DIY check might miss. We recommend professional service for thoroughness and peace of mind.

    Watch out for odd noises, such as grinding or rattling, that point to debris or a failing motor. If the pump runs constantly, fails to turn on or gives off a foul odor from the pit, it's definitely time for a professional check. Visible rust or corrosion are also red flags.

    If your basement is finished or storms often knock out your power, a backup is a wise choice. We install and maintain battery and water-powered backup systems.

    Key signs include strange noises like grinding or rattling, the pump running constantly, failing to turn on, visible rust or corrosion, or water pooling in your basement. Don't wait for a flood to contact us.

    A standard sump pump maintenance visit usually takes about 30 minutes to an hour. If we find issues requiring repair, the time might be extended, but we will always communicate this upfront.

    Most quality pumps last seven to ten years. Pumps that sit dry for long periods can seize sooner, which is why testing matters during dry months.

    We recommend having your sump pump professionally maintained at least once a year. If your home is in an area prone to heavy rainfall or has a high water table, twice-yearly maintenance might be more beneficial. Regular checks confirm it's ready for peak seasons.
