    What Our Leawood, KS Sewer Crews Handle

    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Leawood

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Each fails in its own way. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Commercial Sewer Cleaning in Leawood, KS

    Commercial lines take a beating. Kitchens put grease into the line daily. Offices and retail buildings deal more with paper towels and foreign objects flushed in public restrooms, which snag on joints and catch everything after them, and our heavy jetters handle 6 inch commercial pipe without much trouble.

    Scheduled Sewer Cleaning in Leawood, KS

    History predicts the next backup. Repeat lines get a schedule. A line that has backed up before can be cleaned on a set schedule, often every year or two, so it never stops again without warning.

    Root Removal From Sewer Lines in Leawood

    Trees send roots toward any moisture they can find. A sewer joint is a steady source. We use a sectional cable with a root saw sized to the pipe to cut the mass free and pull it out, then follow with a jetter if fine root hairs are left behind, and a camera shows where roots are entering so you can choose between lining and repair.

    Preventative Sewer Maintenance in Leawood

    Roots come back after cutting. Plan on regrowth in 12 to 18 months. Putting a root-prone main on a yearly cleaning schedule keeps the growth small enough that it never stops the line, and it costs far less than an after-hours emergency call.

    Residential Sewer Cleaning in Leawood

    Household sewers clog slowly. It takes months of buildup to stop a main. Each flush adds a little more to the pile at a rough joint or a low spot, until the 4 inch line is down to a trickle and the lowest drain in the house starts to back up, and we clear it with a sectional cable or a small jetter sized for the pipe.

    Tree Root Removal in Leawood, KS Sewers

    Roots get in through tiny gaps. Then they fill the pipe. We cut the root ball with a saw head sized for the pipe, then flush the leftovers with a jetter when the growth is heavy, and a camera shows where they are getting in.

    Root Masses in Leawood Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Clay joints let roots in. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.
