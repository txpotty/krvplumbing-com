    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Leawood, KS

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Leawood, KS

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. Attic tanks need a working pan.     Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Leawood

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Those are fixable. With a leak, we first check whether water comes from a top fitting or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Electric Element Check in Leawood

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. A failed lower element shows up as hot water that runs out halfway through a shower. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Leawood, KS

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. You get a straight answer on which one is worth it.     Electric Thermostat Service in Leawood

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. A tripped reset is a symptom. We trace the cause, often a shorted element or a stuck thermostat, before resetting it. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    Sediment Flushing in Leawood, KS

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. That layer insulates water from the heat. Our method is short bursts of line pressure that churn up the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Expansion Tank Service in Leawood, KS

Our tech checks the air charge with a tire gauge. The precharge needs to match static pressure in the house. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Plan on 45 to 90 minutes. Thick scale adds time. Tight closets, attics and seized anodes each add a little.

    Three to five years for a magnesium rod is typical on hard water. A softener shortens it further. We measure the rod at each visit and tell you roughly how many years are left, so you replace it before the lining starts to rust rather than after.

    Sediment hardens, the burner or elements work harder, and the bottom of the tank runs hotter than it should. You lose efficiency first.
