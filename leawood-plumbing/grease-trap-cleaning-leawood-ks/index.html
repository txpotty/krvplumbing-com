    Slow sinks come first. Gurgling floor drains and a sour smell by the dish station usually follow. By the time gray water is coming up through a floor sink, the trap is usually well past 25 percent full and grease is already heading down the line.

    Watch the floor sinks during dish rush. Standing water in a floor sink is the first hint. Other signs include a rancid smell near the dish station, grease showing up in the cleanout nearest the street, and drains that gurgle after the dish machine empties.

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps and in-ground gravity interceptors make up most of our work. We also service automatic grease removal units.
