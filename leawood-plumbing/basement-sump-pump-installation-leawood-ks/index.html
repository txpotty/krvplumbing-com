    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Leawood

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Submersibles sit in the pit, sealed, and run quiet. A pedestal mounts the motor on a column above the water, which makes them cheaper and easier to service but louder, so they work best in an unfinished utility room rather than a finished family room. Either type gets the job done.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. We pick the model by horsepower and lift, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Leawood basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.

    We strongly recommend professional installation. You are dealing with wiring, pipe and concrete in one job, and a bad seal on the lid, an undersized pit or a discharge that drains toward the house can leave you worse off than having no pump at all. Mistakes show up in storms.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the check valve, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. The visit is short.

    Labor on our sump pump installs is warrantied, typically for one year. Each pump also has a factory warranty, usually one to five years depending on the brand and model, and we write the exact coverage on your invoice so you know which part to call about if something fails. Keep the receipt.
