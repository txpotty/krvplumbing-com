    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Gardner

    Annual residential testing runs $75 to $150 per assembly. A rebuild usually costs $150 to $450. New residential installs range from $300 to $1,200, and commercial RPZ assemblies often cost $1,500 to $5,000 installed once the drain and enclosure are included.

    Testing must be done by a certified backflow tester using calibrated equipment. Providers reject anything else. A recent calibration date is part of every report.

    Backflow preventers stop contaminated water from flowing backward into the public supply during pressure drops. Irrigation, fire lines and commercial equipment are the most common sources of that contamination, and water providers require assemblies on them to protect public health. It is the law in most places.

    Installation and repair should be left to a licensed plumber who holds tester certification. Installing the wrong device, mounting a PVB too low or burying an RPZ in a pit can each fail inspection and leave the water unprotected. Fines can follow.

    Water dripping or running from the relief valve on an RPZ is the clearest sign. A pressure drop, a split bonnet after cold weather or water with an odd taste can also mean trouble. Have it checked promptly.

    A standard test usually takes 20 to 45 minutes per assembly. We tell you before shutting off the water for the few minutes the test needs. A rebuild adds about an hour.

    You will usually get a notice from the Gardner water department with a repair deadline. We can diagnose the failure, rebuild the checks or relief valve and retest on the same visit in most cases, then file the passing result. Deadlines are firm.

    Water providers may send follow-up notices, charge fees or eventually suspend service. Scheduling early avoids these problems. We can often fit urgent tests in on short notice, even when the deadline is only a few days away.
