    What We Fix on Toilets in Gardner, KS

    Leaky Toilet Repair in Gardner

    Food coloring in the tank is the cheapest test we run. If color shows up in the bowl within fifteen minutes without a flush, water is leaking past the flapper or the flush valve seat, and the toilet is quietly refilling itself all day and night. A new flapper fixes most of these.

    Commercial Toilet Repair in Gardner

    Office and retail restrooms take more flushes in a day than a house sees in a month, so the parts wear faster and fail harder. Most commercial toilets use flushometer valves instead of tanks, and those need diaphragm kits and vacuum breakers from time to time. We carry Sloan and Zurn kits. If downtime is a problem, we can come in before you open or after you lock up.

    Pressure-Assist and Dual-Flush Repair in Gardner

    Pressure-assist toilets use a sealed vessel inside the china that compresses air with incoming water pressure, and the cartridge inside that vessel eventually wears out. You will not find most of these parts at a hardware store. We carry Flushmate cartridges and dual-flush seals for the common TOTO and American Standard models, and we check house pressure, since these units need steady line pressure to flush properly. Low line pressure gives you a weak, half-hearted flush.

    Clogged Toilet Solutions in Gardner, KS

    Paper towels, wipes, dental floss and toys cause most of the repeat clogs we clear, and none of them break down the way toilet paper does. The closet auger usually hooks them and pulls them out. If the toilet clogs again within a week, the problem is further down the line. We check the branch drain and the main next.

    Cracked Tank and Supply Line Repair in Gardner, KS

    Braided supply lines last years, but the plastic nut on the toilet end can crack and a bulge in the braid means the inner hose is about to burst. Every visit includes a look at the supply line. Bulges get replaced on the spot. Old vinyl or solid plastic lines get swapped for braided stainless with metal nuts.

    Weak Flush and Clog Repair in Gardner, KS

    Needing two flushes on a regular basis usually comes down to scale in the rim jets, a clogged siphon jet, a partial blockage in the trapway, or a flush valve that does not open all the way. A gurgle from a nearby sink or tub when you flush points to a vent problem. We clear the trap with an auger and treat the jets with a descaler. Some early 1.6 gallon toilets from the 1990s just flush poorly, and we will say so.

    Running Toilet Repair in Gardner

    A tank that keeps hissing and refilling every few minutes is almost always losing water past the flapper or overfilling into the overflow tube. It adds up fast. We look at the chain, float, flapper and seat before touching a part, then replace only what failed. Most running toilets are quiet again in about thirty minutes.

    Flush Mechanism Repair in Gardner, KS

    A weak flush usually means the tank is not filling to the fill line, the chain has too much slack, or the flapper is closing before the tank empties. We bring the water up to the line stamped on the overflow tube and adjust the chain to about half an inch of slack. Handles break too. A cracked handle arm or a stripped mounting nut takes about five minutes to replace.

    Flapper and fill valve jobs take twenty to forty-five minutes. Pulling the toilet and resetting it on a new wax ring takes about an hour, and a flange repair adds another thirty to sixty minutes depending on what is under the floor. A clog past the trap can take longer.

    Call us and we come back. Repairs are covered by our workmanship guarantee, so if the toilet starts running or leaking again from the same cause inside the warranty period, the return visit and the fix cost you nothing. A defective part is handled under the manufacturer warranty.

    Repair makes sense when the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well with new parts. A cracked bowl or tank usually points to replacement, a toilet that clogs every week no matter what we do, or an old 3.5 gallon model that wastes water on every flush. You get a price for each option.

    A failed wax ring is the usual cause, often helped along by a toilet that rocks or a flange that has cracked or rusted through. Condensation can fool you, though. On a humid day, the tank sweats and drips down the outside, which looks like a base leak but is not one.

    Calcium and magnesium in hard water crust onto the flush valve seat, the fill valve ports and the rim jets under the bowl lip. That leads to slow refills and weak flushes over time. Descaling helps. Fill valves with simpler, larger ports tend to last longer when the water is hard.

    A loose chain or a float set too high is a fair DIY fix, and so is swapping a flapper if you buy the right size for your flush valve. Take the old one to the store. Should it keep running after a new flapper, the seat or the fill valve is usually the problem, and that is a good time to call KRV Plumbing.
