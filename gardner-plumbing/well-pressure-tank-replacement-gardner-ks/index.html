    Constant Pressure System Conversion in Gardner, KS

    Constant pressure systems use a variable speed drive that adjusts pump speed to demand. You get even pressure across fixtures. These systems can run on a smaller tank, though the drive and wiring add to the price. We explain the trade-off.

    Well-X-Trol Pressure Tank Installation in Gardner, KS

    Amtrol's Well-X-Trol line uses a diaphragm design. We put in plenty of them. Its stainless or polypropylene water connection keeps water off the steel shell, which helps the tank resist rust from the inside, and the factory pre-charge still gets checked and adjusted to your switch on install.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Gardner

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. We shut off power to the pump first.     Well Pressure Tank Leak Repair vs. Replacement in Gardner

    Leaks at the tank tee, a gauge or a fitting can be fixed. Once the shell leaks, it is done. Bladder tanks with a fixed bladder cannot be rebuilt, so a torn bladder or rusted seam means a new tank, while a leaking tee or relief valve is a simple part swap we can usually do in under an hour.

    Cost-Effective Well Pressure Tank Upgrades in Gardner, KS

    Upgrading a size often costs less than people think. The difference between two tank sizes is usually modest. A larger tank means fewer pump starts per day, and fewer starts is the biggest single thing you can do for pump life, so we often recommend it during a replacement.

    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Gardner, KS

    Diaphragm tanks use a flexible sheet clamped inside the shell. The diaphragm splits air from water. When it fails, symptoms match a bladder tank, with short cycling, pressure swings and water spitting from the air valve. Replacement is the usual answer.

    Once a year, test the pre-charge. Shut off the pump, drain the system to zero, and put a tire gauge on the valve at the top of the tank, topping it up to two psi below the cut-in if it reads low. Water out of the valve means a torn bladder.
