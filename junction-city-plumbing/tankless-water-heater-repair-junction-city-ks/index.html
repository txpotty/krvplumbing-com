    What Our Tankless Plumbers Fix in Junction City, KS

    Navien Tankless Water Heater Repair in Junction City

    Navien condensing units use a secondary stainless exchanger to recover heat from the flue gas. That makes condensate, and a plugged trap or a full neutralizer will shut the heater down with a drain or flue error. We clear the trap, check the PVC vent pitch and test the circulation pump on NPE-A models with built-in recirculation. Then we fire it at low and high flow.

    Tankless Water Heater Descaling Service in Junction City

    Scale is calcium and magnesium carbonate that comes out of hard water as it heats up, and a tankless exchanger heats water fast through narrow passages. Water across much of Kansas runs quite hard, leaving mineral scale in water heaters, faucets, showerheads, and dishwashers. We hook a small pump and hoses to the isolation valves and circulate descaler for 45 to 60 minutes. Then we flush with clean water. The inlet screen gets cleaned too.

    Flow Sensor and Thermistor Repair in Junction City

    Tankless heaters start when the flow sensor sees about half a gallon a minute, then trim the burner using thermistor readings. A dirty inlet screen or a failing sensor shows up as no hot water, temperature swings or a unit that drops out mid-shower. We pull the screen, read sensor values in the service menu and compare thermistor resistance to the chart. Usually under an hour.

    Condensing Tankless Repair in Junction City, KS

    Condensing tankless heaters pull extra heat out of the exhaust and make acidic condensate that has to drain somewhere. That condensate runs through a trap and, where code or the manual calls for it, a neutralizer filled with limestone or marble chips before it reaches the drain. A plugged trap will lock the heater out. We clean the trap, refill the neutralizer and check the PVC vent for sag or the wrong slope.

    Tankless Descaling and Flushing in Junction City

    Rinnai shows an LC code and Navien posts a scale warning when the exchanger runs hot from mineral buildup. Clear the code without flushing and it comes right back, because the scale is still in there. We connect a circulation pump to the service valves, circulate descaling solution through the exchanger for close to an hour, then flush and clean the inlet screen. Yearly is right for most hard-water homes.

    Ignition Failure Repair in Junction City, KS

    No ignition codes have four usual causes: a dirty flame rod, a bad igniter, low gas pressure or a blocked intake. The gas line is the sneaky one. A tankless burner can need 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, and the half-inch line that fed a 40,000 BTU tank often cannot carry that, which is why it fires at low flow and quits when two showers are on.

    Commercial Tankless Repair in Junction City, KS

    Hotels and gyms burn through hot water all day, so their tankless racks scale up two or three times faster than a home unit. We service rack systems with manifold kits, pulling the fault log and testing the flow sensor on every heater in the rack, and set a descaling schedule by run hours. Quarterly is common.

Replacement is a bigger number. Installed, a new gas tankless typically runs $3,000 to $5,500, so on a unit past 15 years with a failed exchanger we usually lean toward replacing.
