    Common Sink Repairs in Emporia, KS

    Bathroom Sink Repair in Emporia, KS

    Bath sinks have small parts that wear. Pop-up linkage parts sit in a damp cabinet all day, and the pivot nut is the leak we find most often on bathroom sinks. We check pedestal and wall-hung sinks the same way. Vanity sinks just hide the leak better.

    Sink Parts Replacement in Emporia, KS

    Most sinks do not need replacing. A new tailpiece or pop-up assembly costs a fraction of a new sink and installs in an hour, and the bowl and faucet you already like stay right where they are. We match finish where we can. Discontinued faucets sometimes force a full faucet swap.

    Kitchen Sink Repair in Emporia

    Kitchen sinks take the most abuse in the house. Strainers lose their putty seal, sprayer hoses split inside the cabinet, and disposal flanges vibrate loose over the years. The wet part is the one we fix. A cracked composite or porcelain bowl is a replacement, and we will say so.

    Dripping Faucet Repair in Emporia

    A faucet that drips once a second wastes thousands of gallons a year. Older compression faucets need a washer and seat, and newer cartridge or disc faucets need the cartridge itself or its seals. We carry Moen, Delta, Pfister and Kohler cartridges. Odd brands take a day to order.

    Pop-Up Stopper Repair in Emporia, KS

    Stoppers that will not hold water have a linkage problem. Either the clevis slipped on the lift rod or hair has wrapped the stopper so it will not seat. Cleaning fixes half of them. The other half get a new pop-up drain in a matching finish.

    Office and Restaurant Sink Repair in Emporia

    Office breakroom sinks get abused in ways a home sink never sees. Coffee grounds and paper towels go down all day, and nobody checks the trap until water is on the carpet. A strainer basket goes in after we clear it. For restaurants, we keep three-bowl sinks running between the lunch and dinner rush.

    Recurring Sink Clog Repair in Emporia, KS

    Monthly clogs have a cause past the trap. We run a camera or a longer cable into the wall arm and look for a sagging line or buildup where the sink ties into the tub drain. Now and then it is a plugged vent. A sink that gurgles as it drains is the giveaway.
