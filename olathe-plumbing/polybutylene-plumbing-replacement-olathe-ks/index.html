    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Olathe

    Most poly replacements in Olathe fall between $4,000 and $15,000 or more. Size drives the price. Small homes with one bath and an open attic cost the least, and big multi-bath houses or copper jobs cost the most. The quote comes in writing at the visit.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX is flexible, handles freezing and movement better and installs faster, while copper is rigid, tolerates heat and sunlight and has a long track record, so the right answer depends on budget, water and preference.

    Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Hard water does not cause poly to fail the way chlorine does. It adds scale. Scale buildup can reduce flow and add stress to pipe that is already brittle, so in hard water areas we often suggest adding a softener loop during the replacement.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Leaks at fittings are another. Poly often looks fine right up until it fails, so if your home was built between about 1978 and 1995 and has this pipe, replacement is recommended even without a leak yet.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. We use Uponor most often. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.
