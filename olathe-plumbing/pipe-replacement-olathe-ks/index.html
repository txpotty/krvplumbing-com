    Polybutylene Pipe Replacement in Olathe

    Polybutylene is gray or blue plastic installed from roughly the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Chlorine breaks it down over time. Failures tend to be sudden splits at fittings rather than slow drips, and some insurers refuse to cover homes that still have it. Replace it.

    Commercial Pipe Replacement in Olathe

    Commercial buildings often have larger supply lines and more fixtures. Downtime costs money. We plan pipe replacement in phases whenever the layout allows it, keeping restrooms and kitchens running while we work on other sections. Night work available.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair and Replacement in Olathe

    Water under a slab shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a sound of running water, or a meter that spins with everything off. Finding the exact spot comes first. Then you have a choice between breaking the slab to fix that spot or rerouting the line through walls or the attic, and rerouting often costs less and avoids future slab leaks on that line. We explain both.

    PEX Repiping in Olathe

    PEX repipes go faster than copper. Flexible lines snake through walls with fewer openings, and home-run systems from a manifold let you shut off individual fixtures without killing water to the house. Popular choice.

    Whole-Home Repiping in Olathe

    Repiping sounds bigger than it is. You can usually stay in the house. We work room by room, restore water most evenings, and keep dust down with plastic sheeting and drop cloths, finishing with smooth patches ready for paint. Clean finish.

    Water Line Replacement vs. Repair in Olathe, KS

    A single bad spot can usually be fixed. Several leaks in different rooms, rusty water throughout, or pipe that crumbles when we cut it means the rest is close behind. We show you what we find.

    Copper Pinhole Leak Replacement in Olathe, KS

    Pinhole leaks start small. A drip, then a stain. They usually mean the copper wall has thinned from corrosion, so finding one often means others are close. Repipe or patch, we explain.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Olathe

    PEX costs less than copper and goes in faster. It resists scale and corrosion. Private wells serve many rural homes, and minerals, sediment, and nitrate levels make periodic well testing a good habit. We use PEX-a with expansion fittings or PEX-b with crimp rings depending on the job, and we run it in home runs from a manifold or in trunk-and-branch layouts. Fewer joints, fewer leaks.
