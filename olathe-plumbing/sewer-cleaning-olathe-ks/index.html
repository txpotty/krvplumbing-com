    Dealing with Grease Buildup in Olathe, KS

    Grease hardens as it cools. Then it narrows the pipe. A greasy main slowly loses diameter until it cannot carry a full washer load or a bathtub draining at once, and jetting is the right fix.

    Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Olathe, KS

    Cast iron rots from the inside. A lot of Kansas housing dates from the mid-century building boom, with galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains. The bottom of the pipe wears first. Aggressive cutters can break through thin spots, so we use descaling chains and careful jetting made for older iron, which restores flow and gives a clear view of the remaining pipe wall on camera.

    Root Masses in Olathe Sewer Laterals

    Mature trees and old clay laterals do not mix. Clay joints let roots in. We cut roots in several passes, then look on camera to judge the damage and talk about lining or a spot repair.

    Sewer Camera Inspections in Olathe, KS

    On a repeat backup, the camera goes in first. Guessing costs money. The camera feed shows roots, grease, breaks and bellies in real time, and the locator marks the spot on the ground above.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Olathe, KS

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to extend the life of their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Over years the opening shrinks. A cable can punch a hole through the crust, but a jetter cleans the walls back to full size and leaves less for new grease to grab onto, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.

    Preventative Sewer Maintenance in Olathe

    Roots come back after cutting. Usually within a year or so. Putting a root-prone main on a yearly cleaning schedule keeps the growth small enough that it never stops the line, and it costs far less than an after-hours emergency call.

    Root Removal From Sewer Lines in Olathe

    Trees send roots toward any moisture they can find. A sewer joint is a steady source. We use a sectional cable with a root saw sized to the pipe to cut the mass free and pull it out, then follow with a jetter if fine root hairs are left behind, and a camera shows where roots are entering so you can choose between lining and repair.
