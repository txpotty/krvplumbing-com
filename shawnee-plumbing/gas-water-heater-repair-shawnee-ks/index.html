    Pilot Light & Burner Issues in Shawnee

    A pilot that lights, holds for ten seconds and then goes out is almost always a thermocouple or a weak magnet in the gas valve. A pilot that will not light at all is more often a clogged orifice or a dead igniter. Burner trouble looks different. The pilot stays lit but the main flame never comes on, which points to the thermostat side of the valve, a tripped thermal cutoff or a flame arrestor so clogged with lint that the burner starves for air. Each part gets tested in that sequence.

    Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Shawnee

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.

    Burner Cleaning and Repair in Shawnee, KS

    Yellow flame tips, soot on the burner door or a roaring sound at startup all mean the burner is not getting clean air. We pull the burner, brush the ports and blow out the orifice. We also confirm the orifice is drilled for the fuel you have, because a natural gas orifice on a propane heater, or the reverse, will soot up the flue and push carbon monoxide levels well past normal. You want a steady blue flame. Rust flakes on the burner tell us to look up the flue.

    Gas Water Heater Noise Diagnosis in Shawnee

    Rumbling or popping from the bottom of the tank is water boiling under a layer of sediment. Hard water speeds that up, and so does a tank that has never been flushed. We drain the tank through the bottom valve, stir up the settled scale with short bursts of cold water and keep going until it runs clear, which on a neglected tank can take a while. A high whistle is different. A relief valve or a half-closed supply valve is the usual cause.

    Gas Valve and Thermostat Repair in Shawnee, KS

    Lukewarm showers and a tank that never catches up often trace back to the gas control. The tech checks the dial, the immersion sensor and the outlet pressure with a manometer. On most residential tanks the thermostat lives inside the gas valve body, so a bad sensor or a stuck magnet usually means replacing the whole control rather than one small part. That swap takes about an hour. The new valve gets a leak test at every joint.

    Tank Leaks and Pressure Problems in Shawnee, KS

    Water on the floor near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A lot of the time it is the drain valve, the T&P valve or a dripping supply fitting. A relief valve that weeps after every heating cycle usually points to high street pressure or a missing expansion tank, and both are cheaper to fix than a water heater in a Shawnee, KS home. If the bottom seam is wet, the tank has failed. Nothing patches that.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Shawnee

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. Propane units need a matching orifice and a control set near 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. We check pressure with a manometer. Wrong parts get swapped for the right ones.

    Venting and Draft Repair in Shawnee, KS

    Atmospheric tanks rely on hot exhaust rising through a metal flue with an upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot. A loose, rusted or sagging section lets exhaust spill back into the house. The vent gets repaired. Each joint gets three screws, the connector gets proper clearance from wood and drywall, and we run the heater for several minutes with a smoke test at the draft hood to watch the exhaust go up. We do not leave with the heater spilling.
