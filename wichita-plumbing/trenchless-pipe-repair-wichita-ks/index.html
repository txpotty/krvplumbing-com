    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Pipe Repair in Wichita

    Expect roughly $80 to $200 per linear foot. Pipe diameter matters. That can look higher than digging at first, but trenchless usually saves money once you count the landscaping, concrete and irrigation an open trench would destroy, and the full estimate comes after an inspection.

    Trenchless repair takes specialized equipment, materials and training. It should be done by an experienced contractor.

    Cast iron, clay, PVC and concrete can all be lined. The pipe's shape counts. If the pipe has collapsed or shifted badly, bursting may suit it better than lining, and the camera tells us which.

    CIPP lining jobs usually take one to three days. Bursting adds a little time. Pipe bursting may take slightly more, depending on length and access, but either one is much faster than open trenching, which can stretch to weeks once excavation and restoration are counted.

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. Bursting replaces it. With CIPP, a resin-soaked liner cures against the old pipe wall, while bursting breaks the old pipe apart and pulls new HDPE into its place, leaving a completely new pipe.

    Compared to digging, yes. Far less dirt is disturbed. Trenchless repair leaves trees, plants and soil in place, and because the old pipe usually stays in the ground as the host, very little material ends up hauled to a landfill.

    Lining and bursting are rated for 50 years or more. The materials last. Epoxy liners and HDPE pipe resist corrosion, root intrusion and chemical breakdown far better than the clay or cast iron they usually replace, which is where that long rating comes from.
