    Pedestal Sump Pumps in Lenexa

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a solid fit for narrow pits in older homes. They usually last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we usually recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Lenexa, KS

    Submersible sump pumps are designed to sit directly in the sump pit, submerged in water, which makes them quieter and less intrusive. They are strong and effective for basements that take on a lot of water. Our team in Lenexa, KS will install your submersible pump with precision, ensuring proper sealing, discharge piping and electrical connections for quiet, dependable operation, which matters most in finished basements.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Lenexa

    Pedestal sump pumps are an excellent choice for narrow or shallow sump pits, as their motor sits above the water level. This design makes them easier to maintain and can sometimes offer a longer lifespan because the motor stays out of the water. KRV Plumbing can properly install pedestal sump pumps in your Lenexa home, with the right sizing and setup for effective water removal without crowding your pit.

    Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Lenexa

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which saves wear on the motor. Many Kansas homeowners let faucets drip and open cabinet doors on the coldest nights to keep pipes from freezing. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the discharge.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Lenexa

    The cost of basement sump pump installation in Lenexa typically ranges from $800 to $2,000, depending on the type of pump, the complexity of the installation and whether a new sump pit needs to be dug. Battery backup systems or long discharge line runs can affect the final price. We provide a detailed, upfront estimate after an on-site assessment.

    It is a good idea to test your sump pump every 2 to 3 months, especially before and after the rainy season. Simply pour a bucket of water into the sump pit to confirm the pump activates, drains the water and shuts off properly. Regular testing helps identify issues early.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it has to be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    In many areas, including Lenexa, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to ensure compliance with local building codes. KRV Plumbing will help you navigate the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed by local codes.

    A submersible sump pump sits entirely within the sump pit, making it quieter and less noticeable. A pedestal sump pump has its motor above the pit, making it easier to access for maintenance and usually more affordable. Both types are effective for removing water from your basement.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, designed to handle everyday water removal. A secondary sump pump, often a battery-powered backup, serves as an emergency system that kicks on if the primary pump fails, loses power or is overwhelmed by excess water. Both work together for the best protection.
