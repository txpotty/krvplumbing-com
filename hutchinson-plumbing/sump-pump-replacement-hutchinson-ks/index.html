    Float Switch and Check Valve Replacement in Hutchinson

    Many sump pump failures start with a stuck float switch or a leaking check valve. A failed check valve lets water flow back into the pit and causes the pump to short cycle. We install new check valves with every pump replacement and verify the switch moves without binding. Vertical float switches work well in tight pits. Tethered floats suit larger pits.

    If storms knock out power in your area or you have finished living space below grade, a backup is a wise investment. A battery unit with a high-water alarm typically runs six to ten hours of intermittent pumping on a full charge.

    Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. Pumps that sit idle for long periods can fail sooner due to stuck switches.

    While it might seem straightforward, professional sump pump replacement is strongly recommended. It involves electrical connections, proper plumbing for the discharge line, and making sure the pump is correctly sized and installed to prevent future flooding and comply with local Hutchinson codes.

    A primary sump pump is your main defense against basement flooding, powered by your home's electricity. A backup sump pump, usually battery-powered or water-powered, is a secondary system that activates if the primary pump fails, loses power or cannot keep up with heavy water inflow. Many Hutchinson homes are better off with a backup system due to storm risks.

    Sump pumps generally have a lifespan of 7 to 10 years. Even if they appear to be working, their efficiency can decrease over time. Regular inspection and testing are crucial, and replacement is advisable as they approach the end of their expected service life.
