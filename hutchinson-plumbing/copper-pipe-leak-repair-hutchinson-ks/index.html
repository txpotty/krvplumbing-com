    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Hutchinson

    A minor, accessible pinhole repair starts around $200. Hidden leaks cost more. Complex repairs like under-slab leaks can run past $2,000, with location, severity and footage of new pipe setting most of the price. We price it after we find it.

    All standard copper used in homes and businesses. Type M, L and K. Our repairs use copper and fittings from established manufacturers that meet ASTM and NSF standards, and press fittings come from major brands built for potable water. No off-brand fittings.

    A visible pinhole or a small section is usually done in two to four hours. Slab work takes longer. Slab leaks or a repipe can take a full day or more, with the method and access setting the pace. We tell you up front.

    Water chemistry is a big factor. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Pressure matters too. Water that is too acidic or too alkaline, aggressive soil around buried pipe, high pressure, poor installation and foundation movement each wear copper down or pull fittings apart. Most leaks have two or three causes.

    Only as a stopgap. Think of it as a bandage. Clamps and epoxy rarely hold on pressurized copper for long, while a patch that gives out inside a wall can cause far more damage than the leak. Get it fixed properly.

    Insurance often covers sudden water damage. Worn pipe is usually excluded. The cost of repairing old or corroded copper is usually yours, but the resulting damage to the house may be claimable. Check with your agent. We provide documentation.

    Clamps and epoxy putty can stop a drip for a day. They will not last. A proper repair means cutting out the thin copper and soldering or pressing in new pipe, and a DIY patch that lets go while you are at work can flood a room in the time it takes to drive home. Treat it as a stopgap.

    If you have had repeated leaks, it is worth pricing. PEX does not pinhole.
