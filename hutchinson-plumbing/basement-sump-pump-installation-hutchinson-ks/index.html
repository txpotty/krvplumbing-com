    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Hutchinson

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    A straight pump replacement usually does not need a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we look up the local rules and file for a permit when one is needed.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. The real answer depends on inflow and lift, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Bigger is not always better.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.

    Severe thunderstorms are common in Kansas, and heavy downpours can overwhelm gutters, storm drains, and older sewer lines. Outages and storms arrive together, so we recommend a battery backup in most basements. It switches on by itself when the main pump loses power, and it also catches the water if the primary switch sticks or the pump simply cannot keep up with a heavy inflow. It is cheap insurance.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Over seven years old is suspect.

    Discharge belongs in the yard, a dry well, or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Most local codes do not allow sump water in the sanitary sewer, and it can draw a fine.
