    Water-Saving Toilet Upgrades in Overland Park, KS

    Older toilets are one of the largest indoor water users in a house, and replacing a 3.5 gallon model with a 1.28 gallon one cuts toilet water use by more than half. Dual-flush models go lower for liquids. Our recommendations lean on high MaP scores so the savings do not come with double flushing.

    Replacing Pressure-Assisted Toilets in Overland Park, KS

    Pressure-assisted toilets use a sealed vessel in the tank that stores air under line pressure and pushes water through the bowl hard, which helps in houses with long, low-slope drain runs. They are loud. When we replace one, we pick a vessel brand with parts that are easy to get later, and we check that house pressure is in the range the unit needs.

    High-Efficiency Toilet Installation in Overland Park, KS

    Toilets made before 1994 often use 3.5 gallons a flush, and some from the 1970s use five or more. A WaterSense model uses 1.28 gallons or less, and the better ones still clear the bowl in one try because the trapway and flush valve are larger than they used to be. Savings show up fast. Check with your water provider about rebates.

    Emergency Toilet Replacement in Overland Park

    A toilet that cracked overnight and is still seeping into the floor cannot wait for next week, so we bring a standard round and elongated model on emergency calls.     Rental and Multi-Unit Toilet Replacement in Overland Park, KS

    Landlords with several units do better standardizing on one toilet model with common parts, because the next flapper or fill valve is then the same part in every unit.     Toilet Flange Repair During Replacement in Overland Park

    Pulling the old toilet is the only chance to see the flange, and a cracked plastic ring or a rusted cast iron flange will ruin a new install within a year. We check it every time. For a cracked flange, a stainless repair ring is often enough, while a flange that sits below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender so the seal compresses properly.

    Cracked Bowl or Tank Replacement in Overland Park

    Sometimes only the tank is cracked, and if the model is still in production, a matching tank costs less than a whole new toilet. First we find the model number inside the tank. If the bowl is the cracked part, the whole toilet gets replaced, since the bowl carries the seal to the floor and cannot be patched.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Overland Park

    A standard toilet replacement usually runs $400 to $800 with the new toilet and labor included, depending mostly on the model you choose. Flange or subfloor repairs add to it. We quote those separately before doing them, so the base number does not move.

    Our short list starts with TOTO Drake, American Standard Champion, Kohler Highline and Gerber Viper, all of which flush well and use parts you can find at most supply houses. Mansfield makes a solid budget pick. We will match the model to your rough-in.

    About two hours for a standard swap. In that time we remove the old toilet, inspect the flange, set the new one and test it, with the old fixture loaded on the truck at the end. A bad flange adds thirty to sixty minutes.

    A like-for-like replacement in the same spot generally does not. Moving drains or adding a bathroom usually does.

    Most swaps take 1.5 to 2.5 hours. If the flange or subfloor needs work, add up to an hour.

    Rough-in is the distance from the finished wall behind the toilet to the center of the drain, which you can find by measuring to the closet bolts. Twelve inches is standard. Older homes sometimes have 10 or 14 inch rough-ins, and a toilet made for the wrong one either sticks out or will not fit.

    Watch for cracks, weekly clogs, a base that rocks after it has been reset, and a tank that uses 3.5 gallons or more. The age of the toilet matters too. Toilets past 25 years old are often worth replacing even if nothing is broken yet.

    It is doable for handy homeowners, but old toilets are heavy and flange problems are common once the bowl comes off. Hiring it out avoids cracked porcelain and leaks under the floor.
