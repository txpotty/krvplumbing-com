    Frequently Asked Questions About Residential Plumbing Service in Overland Park

    Pricing depends on the job. Simple repairs like a faucet cartridge or toilet rebuild tend to cost $150 to $350, a water heater installation is often $1,200 to $3,500, and slab leak repairs or full repipes can reach several thousand dollars. The exact number comes after the diagnosis.

    Start at the water meter. Turn off every fixture and watch the leak indicator, and if it still spins, look for warm spots on the floor, cracks in tile, mildew smell or a water bill that jumped without explanation. A pressure test confirms it.

    Some jobs are fine to DIY. A flapper swap or a trap cleanout is a reasonable weekend task, but gas lines, water heaters and anything inside a wall can turn into a flood, a fire risk or a failed inspection if the work is off. A licensed plumber gets those right and to code.

    Keep grease out of the kitchen drain. Use strainers in tubs and showers, flush only toilet paper, and find your main water shutoff now, before an emergency, so you are not hunting for it with water on the floor. Older homes benefit from a yearly check.

    Swapping a showerhead or a toilet flapper is fine for most people. Leave gas lines, water heaters and in-wall piping to a licensed plumber, for safety and because those jobs usually need a permit and an inspection that a homeowner repair can fail. Thread tape is not a gas repair.

    Soft water and normal pressure prevent a lot of what we see. Keep grease out of the drains and test pressure once a year, since anything over 80 psi wears out supply hoses and fixtures faster than people expect. A yearly inspection catches the rest.

    Emergencies go to the top of the board. We aim to have a plumber at the house within a few hours, and for a burst pipe or sewage backup we will walk you through closing the main shutoff on the phone so the damage stops while we drive. Grab towels and a bucket while you wait.
