    What a Tank Tune-Up Covers in Overland Park, KS

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Overland Park, KS

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not. Private wells serve many rural homes, and minerals, sediment, and nitrates make periodic well testing a good habit. We break the hex head loose, pull the rod and check how much bare wire shows. Six inches of bare wire means replace it.

    Sediment Flush and Descaling in Overland Park

    Calcium carbonate drops out of hard water every time the tank heats, and over a year it can harden into chunks the size of a thumbnail. Those chunks cause the popping. For stubborn scale we use a potable-rated descaler, then flush it clean. A clean bottom puts less heat stress on the seam.

    Rheem and Bradford White Tank Service in Overland Park

    Rheem, Bradford White, AO Smith and State make most of the tanks in local garages, and each has quirks a tech should know before opening a drain valve. Some models hide the anode under the top cover. Bradford White often uses a combo anode in the hot outlet nipple. We carry the right sockets for each brand.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Overland Park, KS

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. A pan under an attic tank is not optional. We confirm the pan drain line is open and runs to a safe spot. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Maintenance Versus Replacement Costs in Overland Park, KS

    A yearly flush costs a small fraction of a new installed tank, which often runs $1,400 to $3,000 or more depending on size and venting. We tell you which one makes sense. Past 10 to 12 years, rust at the base or a seam leak means replacement. Younger tanks with a sound liner are worth maintaining.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Overland Park

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Most of those are quick fixes. With a leak, we first check whether water comes from a top fitting or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Relief Valve and Expansion Tank Testing in Overland Park

    Street pressure over 80 psi is hard on every fixture in the house, and plumbing codes generally call for a pressure reducing valve above that number. We check static pressure at a hose bib. Then we lift the relief valve lever. If the valve opens and reseats cleanly and the expansion tank holds its charge, the tank is safe from pressure spikes.

    Relief Valve Testing in Overland Park

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. We lift the test lever and watch it reseat. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.
