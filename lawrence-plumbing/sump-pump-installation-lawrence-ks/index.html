    Primary Sump Pump Installation in Lawrence

    A primary sump pump is the one doing the daily work, kicking on every time the float rises in the pit after rain or snowmelt. Sizing comes from two measurements we take on site, the pit diameter and the vertical lift to the discharge. Most homes need 1/3 or 1/2 HP. A 1-1/2 inch discharge line handles the flow from either size without straining the motor.

    High Water Alarms and Smart Monitoring in Lawrence

    A high water alarm is a float switch that sounds when the pit rises past normal. Wi-Fi monitors go further and send a phone alert when the pump runs too long, the power drops or the water climbs past a set level. Rentals need these. We add them to new installs and to pumps already in place.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Lawrence, KS

    A dead pump during a storm means the water is already climbing. We carry common pumps on the truck. Figure on two hours for a typical swap. We test it with a full pit before leaving.

    New Sump Pit Installation in Lawrence

    No pit yet? We cut one at the low corner. We backfill around the basin with washed gravel so groundwater has a path in. The slab gets patched with new concrete. Last come the pump, the lid and the discharge line.

    Combination Sump Pump Systems in Lawrence

    Combination units pair a primary pump and a battery backup on one pre-plumbed discharge assembly. Because everything comes assembled on a single riser, they fit into an 18-inch pit that would be too tight for two separate pumps and two check valves. Install time is shorter too. They suit homes that want backup protection but do not want to enlarge the pit.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Lawrence, KS

    Pedestal pumps put the motor on a column above the pit, so the motor never sits in water. You can reach the motor and the switch without fishing anything out of the pit, which helps in a basement where the pit is shallow or tight. They are louder than submersibles. We set the float rod and the discharge so the pump cycles cleanly.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Installation in Lawrence

    Most Lawrence, KS sump pump jobs land between $600 and $1,800. That covers the pump, the check valve and labor. Digging a pit adds cost. We write it all down before work starts.

    Most homeowners are better off hiring it out. An undersized pump, a backward check valve or a discharge that dumps against the foundation can cause the very flooding you were trying to stop. Water and electricity share the pit.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit usually takes two or three hours. A new pit can take a full day.

    Not every home needs one. Homes with basements, crawl spaces, sunken patios or yards that collect runoff get the most from a pump, since storm water has nowhere else to go.

    Most last seven to ten years. Idle pumps fail sooner, so pour a bucket in the pit every few months.

    Submersibles are quieter and stronger, so they suit heavy use. A pedestal pump costs less and you can reach the motor without lifting it out, but it makes more noise and moves less water.

    Have it cleaned and inspected once a year, ideally before the rainy season. We check the float, clean the pit and test the discharge. It catches problems early.

    Test it every month by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Once a year, scoop out silt, check the float for free movement and walk outside to make sure the discharge outlet is clear of leaves and ice. Takes ten minutes.
