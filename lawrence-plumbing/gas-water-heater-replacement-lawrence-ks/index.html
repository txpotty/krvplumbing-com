    40 and 50 Gallon Tank Replacement in Lawrence

    Forty and fifty gallon gas tanks are the workhorse in most Kansas homes, and a leak at the bottom seam means the tank is finished. Newer tanks can run taller than the old one. We check clearance before we order. Plan on about two to three hours.

    No Hot Water Calls in Lawrence

    No hot water at all usually points to a dead thermocouple or a gas control valve that has failed after years of service. Those get tested before anything else. On a tank past ten years, a new heater usually beats a new valve.

    Thermal Expansion Tank Installation in Lawrence

    Closed systems are the reason expansion tanks exist. With a check or regulator on the main, expanding hot water has nowhere to go and the pressure spikes each heating cycle. Many codes require a thermal expansion tank there. We set its air charge to match house pressure.

    Power Vent Water Heater Replacement in Lawrence

    Blower motors and pressure switches are the weak points on power vent tanks. A second blower failure on an older tank is usually the point where a new heater makes more sense. We confirm a dedicated outlet first.

    Bradford White Tank Replacement in Lawrence

    Most of the tanks on our trucks are Bradford White, partly because their Defender combustion system and ICON gas valve are simple to service. We help you match model to demand. Then it gets installed to code.

    Large Capacity 75 Gallon Gas Heaters in Lawrence, KS

    Big families with soaking tubs often outgrow a 50 gallon tank and need a 75 gallon heater or a high-recovery 50 at around 65,000 to 75,000 BTU. A bigger burner needs more gas. We size the gas line before installing, since an undersized line cuts recovery.

    Rheem Gas Water Heater Service in Lawrence, KS

    Rheem tanks and their Ruud twins share most parts, which helps when a gas valve fails a few years in. We install both. We set them up per the factory manual and test draft.

    High-Efficiency Gas Water Heater Upgrades in Lawrence, KS

    Condensing gas tanks run around 90 percent efficient or better and vent through PVC, while a standard atmospheric tank sits closer to 60 percent. You will need a condensate drain and a nearby outlet. Payback depends on usage.
