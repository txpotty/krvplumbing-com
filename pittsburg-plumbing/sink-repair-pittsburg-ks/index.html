    Kitchen Sink Repair in Pittsburg

    Kitchen sinks take the most abuse in the house. Strainers lose their putty seal, sprayer hoses split inside the cabinet, and disposal flanges vibrate loose over the years. We fix the part that is wet. A cracked composite or porcelain bowl is a replacement, and we will say so.

    P-Trap Repair and Replacement in Pittsburg, KS

    The trap holds a plug of water that keeps sewer gas out of the room. Code calls for a trap seal between 2 and 4 inches, and a trap that leaks, sags or was cut down by a past owner can lose that seal and let the smell in. We replace bad traps with a matched kit. Chrome traps that have rusted thin get swapped for PVC or brass.

    Burst Supply Line Repair in Pittsburg

    A burst line under a sink can push out several gallons a minute. Close the angle stop, or the main if the stop fails, because every minute adds water to the subfloor. Then lay towels down. We replace the line and the stop and moisture-check the cabinet.

    Slow or Clogged Sink Drain Repair in Pittsburg, KS

    Slow drains almost always clog at the trap or the first few feet past the wall. Kitchen clogs are grease and food that cooled and stuck, and bathroom clogs are hair wound around the stopper with soap scum holding it together. We clear it with a hand auger. Hydro-jetting is for main lines, not sink arms.

    Emergency Sink Repair in Pittsburg, KS

    Shut the angle stops first. Turn the two valves under the sink to the right, and if they will not hold, close the main shutoff for the house and then call. Active leaks get the next open truck. The plumber brings stops and supply lines to stop it on the first visit.

    Low Water Pressure at the Sink in Pittsburg, KS

    Unscrew the aerator before anything else. Most single-faucet pressure problems are a packed aerator screen, and after that we look at the cartridge and the angle stop for debris. Low pressure everywhere points to a different problem. We test the pressure regulator in that case.

    Leaking P-Trap Fix in Pittsburg

    Check the slip nuts first. Plastic slip nuts crack when they are overtightened with pliers, and the beveled washers inside them flatten until water seeps out at every joint along the trap. We replace the trap as one kit. A 1-1/4 inch kit for bath sinks, 1-1/2 inch for kitchens.
