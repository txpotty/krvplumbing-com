    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Pittsburg, KS

Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Slab Leak Reroutes in Pittsburg

    When one line under the slab leaks, a full repipe is not always the first step. Rerouting one branch overhead and leaving the bad pipe in the slab is often cheaper than both a repipe and a jackhammer repair. It suits a first-time leak. After multiple leaks, we will show you why a full repipe may cost less over the next ten years.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Pittsburg, KS

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Brown water after the house sits overnight, weak flow at upstairs showers and leaks at threaded joints are the usual signs that galvanized is near the end. New PEX or copper takes its place. Owners usually notice stronger flow the first day.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Pittsburg

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Much of Kansas has expansive clay soil that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can shift foundations and stress buried pipes. Instead of breaking concrete to patch one pipe, we reroute new supply lines overhead through the attic and down interior walls, then abandon the old lines in the slab. No jackhammer needed. It ends this leak and takes the rest of the slab plumbing out of the picture.

    Repiping for Hard Water in Pittsburg, KS

    Water across much of Kansas runs quite hard, leaving mineral scale in water heaters, faucets, showerheads, and dishwashers. Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium scale inside pipe, and over years it narrows the bore and speeds up corrosion in metal lines. Pressure suffers. For homes with hard water, we usually recommend PEX-A, which resists scale and does not corrode, and we can add a softener loop or a whole-house filter during the repipe. Planning it now saves a second visit later.

    Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Pittsburg, KS

    Polybutylene, a gray or blue plastic pipe installed in many homes from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s, breaks down with exposure to chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water. It fails without warning. The pipe gets brittle from the inside, and fittings crack, so a home that has had one polybutylene leak is usually at risk everywhere else in the system. Full replacement is the fix. Insurers often ask about it at renewal.
