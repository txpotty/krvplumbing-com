    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Pittsburg, KS

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. Attic tanks need a working pan. We confirm the pan drain line is open and runs to a safe spot. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Gas Venting Tune-Up in Pittsburg, KS

    Atmospheric gas tanks vent by draft alone, so the vent connector needs upward slope of at least a quarter inch per foot and tight joints with three screws each. We check draft at the hood. Lint on the flame arrestor of an FVIR tank can starve the burner and kill the pilot. We vacuum it clean.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Pittsburg

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. We match the new valve to the heater's input rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. A capped discharge pipe is a serious hazard.

    Electric Element Check in Pittsburg

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Pittsburg

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. That alone takes 30 to 40 minutes. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Any weeping at the dielectric nipples goes on the report.

    Emergency Tank Inspections in Pittsburg

    Sudden changes call for a look the same day: a tank that suddenly rumbles, hot water that turns brown, a puddle forming under the pan, or a relief valve that will not stop dripping. We diagnose first. The fix may be a flush and a drain valve, or the liner may be gone.

    Anode Upgrades for Odor in Pittsburg, KS

    A rotten egg smell from the hot side only is almost always sulfate-reducing bacteria reacting with a magnesium anode. Swapping the rod usually fixes it. We replace it with an aluminum-zinc rod or a powered anode, then chlorinate the tank to kill the bacteria living in it. Powered anodes never wear out and draw very little electricity.
