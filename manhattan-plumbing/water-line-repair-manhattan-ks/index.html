    Low water pressure can be a frustrating symptom of an underlying water line problem, such as blockages, leaks, or a failing pressure regulator. In Manhattan, if your water pressure has dropped suddenly, it's crucial to have your main water line inspected. We diagnose the cause of low pressure, whether it's mineral buildup restricting flow or a hidden leak, and perform the necessary repairs or replacements to restore strong, consistent water pressure throughout your home.

    PEX and Copper Water Line Repair in Manhattan

    Whether your home has modern PEX piping or traditional copper lines, our technicians are experienced in repairing both. PEX offers flexibility and corrosion resistance, while copper is known for its durability. We carry a full stock of matching fittings and tools for each material, ensuring a proper, code-compliant repair. Our expertise extends to identifying common failure points for each type, such as fitting issues with PEX or pinhole leaks in older copper lines, frequent in homes with harder water.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in Manhattan, KS

    A full break can flood a yard, wash out soil under walkways and leave your home without water. Our emergency crews shut off the supply, find the break and set up a temporary bypass when the repair will take more than a few hours. Most emergency breaks are repaired on the same day.     Galvanized Water Line Replacement in Manhattan, KS

    Many homes built before the 1970s still have galvanized steel service lines that rust from the inside. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to extend the life of their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Hard water speeds up scale buildup, which restricts flow and causes rust-colored water. Patching galvanized pipe seldom holds up because the surrounding pipe is just as corroded. We usually recommend replacing the full run from meter to house with copper or PEX, which restores pressure and stops repeat leaks.

    Main Water Line Repair in Manhattan

    The main water line brings water from the public supply to your home. When this line leaks or breaks, it can cause significant water loss, foundation damage, and low water pressure. Our team specializes in diagnosing and repairing main water line issues, from pinpoint leaks to full pipe ruptures, so your home's primary water source is secure. We use advanced detection methods to pinpoint the exact location of the problem, minimizing excavation and disruption to your property in Manhattan. We also check for common issues like root intrusion or shifting soil.

    Pressure Regulator and Shutoff Repair in Manhattan

    Many water line problems are made worse by high incoming pressure. A failed pressure reducing valve can push well over 80 psi into your plumbing, which stresses the service line and every fixture. We test static pressure with a gauge and replace worn regulators from brands like Watts and Zurn Wilkins. We also swap seized gate valves for full-port ball valves so you can shut the water off quickly in an emergency.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Manhattan

    If your service line runs under a driveway, pool deck or mature landscaping, trenchless replacement can save a lot of restoration cost. We dig small pits at each end and pull new HDPE or PEX pipe through using a pneumatic boring tool or pipe pulling equipment. The process typically takes one day. You keep your pavers and trees, and the new line carries a lengthy manufacturer warranty.

    Galvanized Pipe Water Line Repair in Manhattan, KS

    Many older homes in Manhattan, KS still have galvanized steel water lines, which are prone to corrosion, rust, and reduced water flow over time. Our plumbers are adept at working with these older systems, carefully assessing the pipe's condition and recommending the most effective repair strategy to extend its life or plan for eventual replacement.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Repair in Manhattan

    The cost of water line repair in Manhattan, KS can vary widely, typically ranging from $500 for a minor leak repair to $5,000 or more for a full main line replacement or complex trenchless work. Factors include the location of the leak, the type of pipe, the extent of damage, and whether excavation is required. We provide a detailed, upfront estimate after a thorough inspection.

    We use only quality, reputable brands for all our water line repairs and installations. This includes trusted names in PEX like Uponor and Viega, and durable copper piping from manufacturers known for their reliability. Our fittings are always code-compliant and selected for their longevity in varied soil and water conditions, for a robust repair.

    Many policies do not cover the pipe itself but may cover resulting damage to your home. Some homeowners carry separate service line coverage through their utility or insurer. We provide photos and an itemized invoice to back up your claim.

    In most communities the water provider owns the meter and the pipe on the street side of it. The homeowner is responsible for the service line from the meter to the house. We check which side the leak is on before quoting you.

    Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Slab leaks and broken sewer laterals are common results of shifting clay, especially after long dry spells followed by heavy rain. Tree roots can also crush or wrap shallow lines, and mineral scale narrows older pipe over time. Aging galvanized pipe is the most common cause in older homes.

    For major water line repairs or replacements, particularly on the main service line connected to the city supply, a plumbing permit is often required in Manhattan. KRV Plumbing handles all necessary permitting on your behalf, so your repair complies with local regulations and passes inspection. This protects you from potential fines and keeps the work up to code.

    We typically install Type K copper, PEX rated for direct burial or HDPE for trenchless pulls. Each holds up well in most soils when properly bedded. We will recommend the right material based on your soil, line length and price range.

    Preventing future water line issues involves a few key steps: being mindful of landscaping near buried lines, quickly fixing minor leaks, and considering a whole-home water pressure regulator if your pressure is consistently high.
