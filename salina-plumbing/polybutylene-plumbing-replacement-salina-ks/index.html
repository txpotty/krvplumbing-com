    Full Home Repiping with PEX in Salina

    Most poly replacements we do use PEX-A, a flexible tubing that runs long distances with few fittings.     Polybutylene Identification and Inspection in Salina

    Polybutylene is usually gray, though it can be white, blue or black, and is often stamped PB2110. Check the heater first. You may also see it under sinks, near the main shutoff or coming through the garage wall, and we trace runs through the attic to confirm how much of the house it serves.

    Fitting Failure and Emergency Repair in Salina

    Acetal plastic and copper crimp ring fittings are often the first part of a poly system to fail. A burst fitting in an attic or wall can cause major damage in minutes. We respond to emergency leaks, make temporary repairs and then schedule a full replacement, because stopping the problem for good means taking the poly system out of service entirely.

    Why Polybutylene Fails in Salina

    Polybutylene reacts with oxidizers like chlorine that water utilities use to disinfect tap water. Over the years, the pipe and its acetal fittings get brittle from the inside, then crack along the inner wall until one splits under normal pressure. Many Kansas homeowners use water softeners to protect their tank water heaters and fixtures against heavy mineral buildup. Heat makes it worse. Most of the failures we see are on hot lines and at fittings close to the heater.
