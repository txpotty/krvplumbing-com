    Cold Water Slab Leak Detection in Salina, KS

    Cold water slab leaks do not create warm spots, so they are more difficult to see. They often show up as a running water meter, damp carpet, a musty smell or water seeping at baseboards. We isolate the cold side and use acoustic equipment to listen for the leak through the slab. Line tracers map the pipe route so we know where to listen.

    Electronic Leak Detection in Salina

    For elusive leaks, our electronic leak detection equipment sends a signal through your pipes and measures changes that indicate a breach. This technology allows us to locate leaks in various pipe materials, including copper, PEX and PVC, commonly found in Salina homes. It helps prevent exploratory digging, saving you time and money.

    Slab Leak Repair Through Direct Access in Salina, KS

    When a single leak is found on otherwise healthy pipe, direct access can be the most economical repair. We typically use PEX for reroutes because it handles heat and resists hard-water corrosion. This option is a good fit when the slab is covered in tile or hardwood.

    A water line slab leak involves pressurized fresh water escaping from supply pipes, often resulting in higher water bills, warm spots or the sound of running water. A sewer line slab leak involves wastewater leaking from drain pipes, which usually causes foul odors, slow drains or visible sewage seepage, and is not usually pressurized.

    Reroutes and repipes often require a plumbing permit, while small direct repairs may not. Requirements vary by city and county. We handle permits and inspections when needed.

    Yes, a persistent slab leak can seriously damage your home's foundation. Continuous saturation of the soil can lead to soil expansion and contraction, causing stress on the concrete slab, leading to cracks, shifting and potentially costly structural repairs.     Common signs of a slab leak in Kansas homes include unexplained increases in water bills, warm spots on your floor, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, damp or mildewy smells and visible cracks in your foundation or walls. Pooling water outside your home near the foundation can also be a clear indicator.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes susceptible to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures. Galvanized steel pipes, though less common under slabs today, can also corrode and leak.
