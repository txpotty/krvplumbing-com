    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Salina

    Most homeowners in Salina, KS spend between $1,500 and $4,000 on a replaced softener, installed. Capacity and brand drive most of it, plus any new loop or prefilter. You get a written quote after the water test.

    Water across much of Kansas runs hard, leaving mineral scale in water heaters, faucets, showerheads, and dishwashers. Hard water carries dissolved calcium and magnesium that drop out as scale wherever water gets heated or sits still for long. Water heaters wear out sooner, and glassware spots. Softening the water prevents it.

    Resin and valves usually give out between 10 and 15 years. Very hard or chlorinated water wears them faster.

    Three to six hours for most homes. That assumes the loop and drain are already in place and no major plumbing changes are needed before the new unit can go in. Soft water is back the same day.

    Sizing comes from two numbers: your hardness in grains per gallon and how many gallons the household uses a day. Multiply them and you get grains per day. We size the resin so it regenerates every few days.

    Use clean pellet salt and keep the brine tank at least a quarter full. Clean out the brine tank every year or two, and break up any salt bridge you find with a broom handle before it stops regeneration completely. An iron prefilter helps on well water.

    Plan on a morning. Disconnecting the old unit, setting the new tanks, making the plumbing connections and running the first regeneration takes three to five hours in most homes. New loops add time.

    Salt-based softeners swap calcium and magnesium for sodium through ion exchange, so the water actually tests soft. Salt-free conditioners leave the minerals in the water but change their structure so they are less likely to stick to pipes and heating elements. Only the salt unit softens.
