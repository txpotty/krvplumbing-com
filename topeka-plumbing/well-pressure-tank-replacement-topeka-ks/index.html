    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Topeka

    Drawdown is the usable water between cut-out and cut-in. A 20-gallon tank might give you five or six gallons. We divide pump flow by drawdown to see how many starts per hour the pump will face, aiming for at least one minute of run time per cycle. That often points to a bigger tank.

    Large Capacity Tanks for Ranch and Commercial Wells in Topeka

    Big properties need big drawdown. Stock tanks, irrigation and barns add up. We install large capacity tanks or banks of tanks on a manifold, sizing them so the pump runs long enough each cycle even under heavy demand. Floor space matters.

    Well-Rite Pressure Tank Installation in Topeka, KS

    Well-Rite tanks use a replaceable diaphragm design in many models. Common sizes are easy for us to get. Like any tank, the key is setting pre-charge before it fills, and checking that drawdown matches the pump. A good tank installed badly fails early.

    Residential Well Pressure Tank Replacement in Topeka

    Most homes on a well have a tank between 20 and 86 gallons sitting near the pressure switch. We shut off power to the pump first. Then we drain the system, unthread the old tank from the tank tee, set the pre-charge on the new one, and plumb it back in with fresh thread sealant and new fittings. Usually a morning.

    Bladder-Style Pressure Tank Replacement in Topeka, KS

    A bladder tank holds water inside a rubber balloon surrounded by compressed air. The air pushes water out when you open a tap. Bladders crack with age, especially if the tank ran with low pre-charge, which waterlogs the tank. Replacement is the fix.

    Bladder Pressure Tank Replacement in Topeka

    Rapid clicking at the switch is the classic sign. That fast on-off pattern means the tank has no usable air, and the pump is now taking every hit of demand directly, which wears the pump and the switch contacts. We swap the tank and the cycling stops.

    Commercial Well Pressure Tank Services in Topeka, KS

    Commercial wells often run several tanks in parallel. Multi-tank setups are common. We check each tank's pre-charge separately, since one bad tank in a bank can still cause short cycling, while planning the work so the building keeps water. Larger tanks need two people.

    Switch contacts pit and burn over time. Worn contacts can fail to open or close. When we swap a tank we look at the contacts and the sensing port underneath, and we usually recommend replacing a switch older than the tank it served. Cheap part.
