    Watch for multiple drains backing up at once, gurgling toilets, sewage smells in the yard and wet or unusually green patches over the line. Water backing up into a tub or shower when you run the washing machine is another classic warning sign.

    Yes, weather can affect sewer lines. Strong winds and dry spells can pull moisture out of the soil, while sudden cold fronts can drop temperatures sharply overnight. Heavy rains can saturate the ground, putting pressure on pipes and widening existing cracks. Dry spells can cause soil to shift, leading to pipe misalignment or breaks.

    The duration of a sewer repair depends heavily on the scope of work. Simple spot repairs or hydro-jetting might take a few hours. Trenchless repairs like pipe lining can often be completed in one to two days. Full sewer line replacements involving excavation can take anywhere from two to five days, depending on the length and complexity of the line.

    To prevent future sewer line problems, avoid flushing anything other than toilet paper, dispose of grease properly and be mindful of what goes down your drains.
