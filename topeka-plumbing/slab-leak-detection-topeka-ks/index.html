    Pressure Testing and Isolation in Topeka

    We always start with isolation. Our tech closes the main, caps or valves off the hot and cold sides, and puts a gauge on each, then watches which side loses pressure over a set time.     Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in Topeka

    Expect $250 to $700 for slab leak detection in Topeka. Tougher leaks cost more. The fee covers pressure isolation, line tracing, acoustic and thermal work, and tracer gas when a plastic line is too quiet for the microphone.

    Direct repair works for one leak on healthy pipe in a reachable spot, usually where the floor covering is cheap to patch, like carpet or vinyl. Rerouting is often better under tile or hardwood, and it also avoids future leaks on that whole line. You get prices on both.

    Locating takes one to three hours. A spot repair or reroute is usually one day. Full repipes take two to four days, depending on the number of bathrooms and how easy the attic is to work in.

    Figure on one to three hours for most homes. Tricky leaks take longer. The search depends on how the pipe is routed, the floor covering and whether the line is copper or PEX, since plastic pipe is quieter and may need tracer gas to confirm the spot.

    Many policies cover access to the leak and the water damage, though not always the pipe itself that failed under the slab. Every insurer is different. Our report includes photos and readings for your claim.

    Slab leaks and broken sewer laterals are common results of shifting clay, especially after long dry spells followed by heavy rain. Water chemistry can also thin copper from the inside. Hot lines expand and rub against concrete or gravel, and over years that wear opens pinholes and cracks.

    High water bills, warm floor spots and the sound of running water with fixtures off are the big three. So do new cracks. Damp carpet, a sudden drop in pressure and a musty odor near baseboards also point to moisture under the slab.

    A water line leak is pressurized, so it runs constantly, moves the meter and can warm the floor if it is on the hot side. Sewer leaks work differently. They leak only when water goes down, causing odors, slow drains or damp spots near bathrooms.
