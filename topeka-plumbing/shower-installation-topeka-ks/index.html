    Prefab Shower Unit Installation in Topeka, KS

    Prefab units are the fastest route to a new shower. The base goes on mortar, the drain and valve get connected and the walls are fastened so they stay rigid. They suit guest baths well. Usually a one-day job.

    Rain and Multi-Head Shower Installation in Topeka

    Rain heads pull up to 2.5 gallons per minute each. Add a handheld and a couple of body sprays and you can be running more water than a standard 40-gallon water heater can keep hot for a long shower. The water heater gets sized up. Thermostatic valves with volume controls run the outlets.

    Tub and Shower Combo Installation in Topeka

    Setting a tub is mostly about level and support. Acrylic and steel tubs get mortar or foam underneath, while cast iron rests on a level ledger, which keeps the caulk line intact. The valve goes in next. We set the spout and head heights to your layout and test the overflow gasket with water.

    New Construction Shower Installation in Topeka, KS

    New homes get their shower plumbing during framing. We rough in valves, supply lines and drains once the walls are framed, set each valve to the depth of the planned finish and locate the drain for the pan you picked. Inspection comes next. Trim goes on once tile is done.

    Digital Shower Systems in Topeka, KS

    Digital shower systems like U by Moen and Kohler DTV need a little more planning. These valves usually sit in a nearby closet or vanity and need low-voltage wiring to the controller and an outlet close by. An electrician handles the outlet. We plumb the valve and outlets.

    Shower Stall Installation in Topeka

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. Fiberglass and acrylic bases go down on a mortar bed so they cannot flex, and flex is the usual reason a base cracks early. Then the drain goes in. We check the flow at the head once the trim is on.

    Curbless Shower Installation in Topeka

    Curbless showers need the floor lowered in the shower area. On a wood floor, we drop the joists or use a recessed pan system, and on a slab, we cut and lower the concrete so the finished floor runs flat into the shower. A linear drain often works best. We plan the slope with your contractor before demo.

    Shower Pan Installation in Topeka

    Shower pans fail quietly. A pan with a pinhole in the liner or a poorly clamped drain will hold water for years while it slowly soaks the subfloor, so we flood-test every tiled pan for 24 hours before tile goes down. Prefab pans get a level check. Both kinds get slope toward the drain.

    Homes around Topeka range from older houses with unusual plumbing to new builds with PEX manifolds. We have adapters and fittings for copper, CPVC, PEX and cast iron drains, so tying a new shower into old plumbing rarely holds up the job. We check the old drain first.
