    Cleaning vs. Repairing a Sewer Line in Topeka, KS

    A sound pipe with a grease or root clog just needs cleaning. Cracked pipe is different.     Hydro-Jetting for Stubborn Clogs in Topeka

    Some lines get cabled three times a year and still clog. The cable is not the fix. Those lines are usually coated in grease or scale, and a cable just cuts a small hole that closes back up. Jetting removes the coating down to the pipe wall. The results hold up far better.

    Residential Clogged Sewer Lines in Topeka

    Most home sewer clogs come from three things: roots, grease and wipes. Many times they team up. Grease coats the pipe, wipes snag on the coating and roots growing through a joint hold the whole mass in place until the line stops. We clear it with a sewer machine, then check on camera to see the main cause.

    Tree Root Invasion Removal in Topeka

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture in the soil. They only need a tiny opening. Once inside, they spread into a net that traps everything moving through. We cut them out with a root saw on a sectional machine, then camera the line to see how many joints are letting roots in. Lining or replacement seals the joints for good.

    Identifying and Repairing Damaged Pipes in Topeka

    A clog that keeps coming back usually means broken pipe. No amount of clearing fixes a crack. Our camera finds the crack, offset or collapse, while the locator marks it so the repair can be quoted right. KRV Plumbing gives you options from a single dig to full replacement.

    Hydro-Jetting Services in Topeka, KS

    Jetting sends water at thousands of psi through a nozzle that sprays backward and drives itself down the pipe. The pipe gets cleaned all the way around. A cable punches a hole through a grease plug, while a jetter removes the grease, scale and fine roots coating the pipe so the next clog has nothing to start on. The camera goes in before the jetter. Badly cracked pipe gets a gentler approach.

    Main Line Hydro Jetting in Topeka

    A jetter cleans the full diameter of the pipe, not just a hole through the middle. That matters a lot. Water at thousands of psi cuts roots, grease and scale, and the rear-facing spray carries all of it downstream as the nozzle pulls itself along the line. We camera the pipe first so jetting only goes into lines that can take it. Results often last years.
