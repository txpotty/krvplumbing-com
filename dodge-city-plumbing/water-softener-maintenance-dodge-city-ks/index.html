    What Our Water Softener Maintenance Covers in Dodge City, KS

    Resin Bed Checks and Regeneration Settings in Dodge City

    Resin does the actual softening. The beads swap sodium for calcium and magnesium, and once they are loaded up they need brine to recharge. We compare the softened hardness against the gallons used since the last regeneration to tell whether the bed is still at full strength.

    Softener Brands We Work On in Dodge City

    Most softeners sold in the last 20 years run on a handful of valve platforms, mainly Fleck, Clack, Autotrol and a few proprietary heads. The valve matters more than the brand. Kinetico and Culligan build their own valves, so we check part availability before quoting.

    Control Valve and Injector Cleaning in Dodge City, KS

    Brine draw depends on a venturi injector, a tiny nozzle and throat that pulls brine out of the salt tank as water rushes past. A few specks of grit can stop it cold. We pull the injector and screen, clean them, and replace the seals and spacers on valves like the Fleck 5600 and Clack WS1 when they show wear.

    Control Valve Diagnostics and Repair in Dodge City

    Leaks at the valve body, water running to drain around the clock, a motor that hums without moving or a display stuck on one cycle tend to mean the seal and spacer stack is worn out. On a Fleck 5600 the fix is a piston and seal kit swap, often done in under an hour without pulling the valve off the tank. Cheaper than a new head.

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Dodge City, KS

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. The tank gets emptied, scrubbed and rinsed. Then we look for cracks near the overflow elbow and the brine well and refill with fresh salt.

    Salt Bridge Removal and Brine Tank Cleaning in Dodge City, KS

    Humidity and cheap salt cause most salt bridges. A hard shell forms across the tank, and the salt underneath dissolves away, leaving a hollow. The softener keeps regenerating on schedule, but it draws plain water instead of brine, which leaves the resin without a recharge and hard water flows to every tap.

    Repair or Replace a Softener in Dodge City, KS

    New softener systems often run $1,200 to $3,500 installed, while a resin rebed usually lands between $300 and $700. If the tank and valve are in good shape, keeping them makes sense. We will tell you plainly to replace it when the valve is obsolete and parts are gone, or when the tank itself is cracked.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Maintenance in Dodge City

    Standard visits typically cost $125 to $250. A valve rebuild adds roughly $100 to $250. Resin replacement is the big-ticket item at $300 to $700, and it still costs far less than a new softener.

    Routine maintenance and small repairs generally do not need a permit. Replacing the whole system or changing the plumbing around it can, based on what the local building department requires, and we handle that paperwork when it applies.

    Evaporated salt pellets are the cleanest option and cause fewer bridges and less sludge than rock salt. Solar crystals land somewhere in the middle. Potassium chloride works if you want to cut sodium, but it softens less per pound, so the salt dose usually has to go up by a quarter or more.

    Once a year works for most homes. Very hard water or a heavily used older unit can justify a visit every six months, especially in a house with a tankless water heater to protect. Between visits, look in the brine tank once a month and watch for spots on glassware.

    A few inches of water can be normal, depending on the design. A tank that keeps filling higher usually has a clogged injector, a stuck float, a bad refill flow control or a drain line restriction. We fix whichever one it is and reset the valve.

    Spots on glassware usually show up first. Towels turn stiff and soap seems to take more to work up a lather. Another tell is a salt level that never goes down, which means the softener is not drawing brine, or a hum from the valve with no cycle change.

    Most visits run about an hour, including both hardness tests and a manual regeneration. Rebedding the resin tank tacks on about an hour more.

    Scale coming back is the giveaway. You may also notice dull laundry and dry skin. Higher salt use, a brine tank full of water, a grinding noise from the valve head or lower pressure at the faucets can also mean the softener needs service.
