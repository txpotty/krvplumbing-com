    Hard Water Faucet Repair in Dodge City

    Scale is the biggest reason faucet seals wear early, because the mineral crust scratches rubber and keeps ceramic discs from closing flat. It happens faster with hard water. The valve body gets cleaned while the old part is out, so the new cartridge or washer seals against a smooth surface.

    Delta and Moen Faucet Repair in Dodge City, KS

    Delta and Moen are the faucets we repair most, and both back their cartridges with lifetime warranties for the original owner on many models. Ask us to check. Kohler, Pfister and American Standard each use proprietary cartridges, and we stock the common ones for each brand.

    Ball Faucet Repair in Dodge City, KS

    Ball faucets have a single lever over a domed cap, and the drip usually comes from the two small rubber seats and springs that press against the ball. A seats and springs kit fixes most of them. Loose handles happen too. If the handle leaks, snugging the adjusting ring under the cap often fixes it.

    Specialty Faucet Repair in Dodge City, KS

    Pot fillers, laundry tub faucets and wall-mount utility faucets each have their own valve styles, and many use compression stems or quarter-turn cartridges that hardware stores do not stock. The common stems ride on the truck. For filtered water faucets, we replace the cartridge or the check valve as needed.

    Dripping Faucet Repair in Dodge City

    One drip a second adds up to more than 3,000 gallons a year, roughly the water used in 180 showers. That waste lands on your water bill. We find the worn washer, O-ring or cartridge and replace it, then check the cabinet below for swelling or stains from the slow leak.

    Faucet Repair Versus Replacement in Dodge City, KS

    A new cartridge usually costs well under the price of a new faucet and can add years of life, as long as the faucet body is sound and parts are still made. Cracked or discontinued faucets are different. We give you both prices so the choice is yours.

    Low Water Flow Faucet Repair in Dodge City

    Sputtering flow, where the water spits and surges, often means air or debris in the line after a water main repair, or a partly clogged cartridge screen. Flushing usually clears it. On pull-down faucets, the check valves in the hose can stick too, and we clean or replace those.

    Disc Faucet Repair in Dodge City

    Ceramic disc faucets last a long time, but grit from a water main repair or scale from hard water can scratch the discs or wear out the rubber inlet seals underneath. The seals are cheap. A scratched disc means a new cartridge, since the two discs are lapped to fit each other and cannot be resurfaced.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in Dodge City

    Most faucet repairs in Dodge City run $120 to $350, depending on the valve type and the parts, with a washer job at the low end and a high-end cartridge at the top. Touchless faucets can cost more. You get a written price before any work.

    Repair means replacing worn parts inside the faucet you have, like washers, seats, O-rings or a cartridge, while replacement means a whole new faucet on the sink. Repair is usually cheaper. A cracked or discontinued faucet is better replaced.

    One to two hours at the most for a standard repair with common parts. An ordered part may mean a short second trip. One visit is the goal, and the truck carries enough stock that most repairs never need a second trip to the supply house.

    A drip from the spout, water around the handle or base, a handle that is stiff or loose, and a temperature that drifts while you use it are the usual signs. Noises like squealing or banging count as well. Low flow from one faucet is another.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If we have to order a part, there may be a quick second trip.

    At one faucet, it is usually a clogged aerator or a cartridge packed with scale. When every fixture is weak, look elsewhere, like a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed main valve or old galvanized pipe that has narrowed inside over decades of corrosion. A pressure gauge on a hose bib tells us a lot.

    Low pressure at a single faucet is almost always the aerator or the cartridge. If it is low everywhere, look at the pressure regulator, the supply or aging pipe.

    A slow drip is not, though it should be fixed soon to stop the waste. A faucet stuck open or a split faucet body under the sink is, and the first step is closing the stops below the sink or the main shutoff for the house. We move those calls to the front.
