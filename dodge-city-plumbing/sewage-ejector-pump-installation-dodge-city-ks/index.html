    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Dodge City

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. Never mix the two.

    Duplex and Commercial Ejector Systems in Dodge City, KS

    Commercial buildings, multi-unit properties and busy homes often need duplex systems with an alternating control panel. Alternating the lead pump spreads wear, and when one fails or flow spikes, the second starts on its own and the panel sounds an alarm. Redundancy matters here.

    Emergency Sewage Ejector Pump Replacement in Dodge City

    An ejector failure often starts with the high water alarm, followed by slow drains and a smell near the pit. Stop all use of the basement fixtures and call, because once a plumber is on site most emergency replacements take just a few hours. We carry common pumps.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Dodge City

    Digging the pit means cutting the slab and excavating below it, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Much of Kansas has expansive clay soil that swells when wet and shrinks in drought, which can move foundations and stress buried pipes. We bed the basin on gravel and backfill around it so it cannot float when groundwater rises, then pour new concrete to seal the floor and lock the basin in place. A floating basin breaks pipe.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin Installation in Dodge City, KS

    The basin holds everything, and a 30-gallon size is common for a single basement bathroom. Bigger basins let the pump run longer, fewer times a day, which is easier on the motor and switch, so we size up when the pit serves a laundry or several fixtures. Every lid gets sealed.
