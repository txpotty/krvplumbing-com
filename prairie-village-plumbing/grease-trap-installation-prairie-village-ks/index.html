    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Install in Prairie Village, KS

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Prairie Village, KS

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. You can put one under a sink, beside a dish station or in a pit in the slab. The flow control is not optional. Skip it and a full sink drain overruns the rating, which sends grease right past the baffles. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Prairie Village

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where. Changes get expensive. Moving a trap after the concrete cures means cutting the floor, so we catch problems while it is still paper.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Prairie Village

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. The tank holds water for roughly half an hour, which gives grease time to rise and food solids time to drop. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Grease Trap Replacement and Upgrades in Prairie Village

    Plastic and steel traps both wear out, just in different ways. With steel, rust eats the seams and the welded baffles first. Plastic cracks. We remove the old unit, set the new one in the same spot when it fits, and use no-hub couplings so future work is easier.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Prairie Village

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. The purchase price is higher than a passive trap. Plan for a dedicated circuit near the unit, which your electrician will need to run. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    Emergency Grease Trap Services in Prairie Village, KS

    A grease trap backing up into the floor sinks at the start of Friday dinner service means a closed kitchen and a health inspector who may not let you reopen until it is fixed. We send a tech with a jetter and a camera to find out whether the trap or the line on either side is the problem. Often it is the outlet. A failed trap can often get a stopgap unit that night and a proper replacement later in the week.

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Prairie Village, KS

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. Long runs to the trap let grease cool and coat the line, so we keep the trap as close to the fixtures as we can. Closer is better. For outdoor tanks we measure the hose run from where a pump truck can park.

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Prairie Village

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. Our sizing starts from your peak flow, the worst ten minutes of the night, rather than a daily average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. The install covers setting the unit, piping the inlet and outlet, and adding the vented flow control the maker specifies.

A tank under a parking lot needs a traffic-rated lid and asphalt repair, which a tank in a grass strip does not.

    That depends on the code your jurisdiction follows and the type of trap. Some programs require dish machines to drain through the interceptor, while others keep high-temperature discharge away from certain hydromechanical units because hot water and detergent can push emulsified grease through. We check before we pipe it. Garbage disposers are usually not allowed to drain into a grease interceptor.

    Watch the pump-out bills. A trap that needs service far more often than it used to is either too small or broken inside. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.

    Coverage comes from two places. Your trap carries a factory warranty that varies by brand and material. Our written labor warranty covers the joints we made and how we set the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification.
