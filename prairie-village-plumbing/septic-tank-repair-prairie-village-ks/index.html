    Small repairs like a broken tee or a stuck float usually run $300 to $700. Pump replacements land between $900 and $2,000, floats included, and replacing a drain field often runs $5,000 to $15,000 or higher, based on soil and size. You get the number after we diagnose it.

    Rotten-egg odor near the lid or the field is the giveaway most people notice first. Inside, toilets flush slowly and the tub gurgles. Out in the yard, look for wet spots or puddles above the field lines during a long dry spell. Backups into the lowest drain come last.

    The gas in a septic tank is mostly methane and hydrogen sulfide, and leaning over an open lid can knock you unconscious. Every year people die in tanks, usually reaching for a dropped tool. A concrete lid can weigh well over a hundred pounds. Leave the lid to us.

    Get the tank pumped every three to five years and have the effluent filter cleaned at the very same visit. Repair any toilet that runs or faucet that drips as soon as you notice. A single leaking flapper can push hundreds of gallons a day into the field. Keep wipes out.

    Too little pumping is behind most early failures, because the sludge layer creeps up until it washes out the outlet. Roots and heavy trucks over the field speed it up. Large doses of bleach or chemical drain cleaner kill off the bacteria doing the work. Dense soil can add to it.
